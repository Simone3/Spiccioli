Gross is the payslip's own totale lordo line, typed as printed — the one column here whose meaning comes from the document rather than from the specification, since what a payroll puts in that line varies. Nothing divides a net figure by it. Everything reading gross — its total above, the averages chart, Gross / hour — is a sum, a mean or a rate over that one column, so whatever the line contains they stay consistent with each other and across years. There is no net-to-gross ratio: it was the only figure that mixed an exactly-defined number with a copied one, and it is gone (§15).

Net payment is the one figure on a payslip that may be negative, when a December's year-end tax recalculation comes to more than the month's net. It is entered as printed; net salary follows it down, and check 4 looks for a debit of that amount — or reports the payslip, if the employer carried the debt into the next month instead of taking the money back.
