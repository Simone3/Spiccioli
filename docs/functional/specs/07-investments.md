# §7 — Investments

*[Index](../README.md) · [why it is this way](../why/07-investments.md) · [mockups for this section](../mockups/07-investments.html)*

Four tabs: Holdings, Purchases, Sales, Securities. Holdings is entirely derived from the middle two; Securities is what all three point at.

---

## 7.1 Holdings

> **Mockup —** [Holdings tab](../mockups/07-investments.html#holdings)

- One row per (security, brokerage account) holding a position — quantity greater than 0, and a running quantity that never went below it ([§2](02-domain-model.md)). **Every figure is derived and every column is read-only**, the price and its date included: **nothing on this tab writes to the file.**
- **Ordered by the security's `ticker`, alphabetically**, then by account name where one security is held at two brokers and is therefore two rows ([§2](02-domain-model.md)). Case- and accent-insensitive, like every other comparison of text in the application.
- **A price belongs to the security and not to the holding** ([§7.6](#76-prices)), so this tab states the price and the Securities tab is where it is kept — the whole history, the form that writes one, and *Update prices* ([§7.4](#74-securities)). The same instrument held at two institutions is two rows and one price, which is exactly why the figure is not editable on a row: there is no such thing as *this holding's* price to change.
- **The price cell is the way there.** It is a link, and following it opens the Securities tab on this row's security with its price history beside the list ([§7.4](#74-securities)) — the one place a price is written, corrected or deleted. A cell reading *none* links the same way, that being the row most in need of it.
- **Last priced** sits beside the price and carries **the date of the security's latest Price record** — the as-of date, not the day it was typed ([§2](02-domain-model.md)). It is the same date the price form writes, so a price saved dated today makes the cell read today, and a price fetched for Friday makes it read Friday ([§7.6](#76-prices)).
- **A date older than `priceStalenessDays` is highlighted in the cell itself**, on the row and not only in [§9](09-checks.md). There is no icon and no legend to decode: the date is the thing that has gone wrong, so the date is what is marked, and the row says *how* stale rather than only *that* it is. **A security with no price at all is marked differently rather than more loudly**: its price cell reads *none*, *Last priced* reads an em dash, and its **value reads what the position cost while its gain reads `€ 0,00`**, the row being carried at cost ([§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)). **The two money columns therefore look like a position that has gone nowhere, and the price cell is what says it has not been measured** — the loud marker is check 3 and the Portfolio banner behind it ([§9](09-checks.md)), which is where it belongs: a row cannot shout, and a banner can. The em dash is not highlighted — there is no date to age, and the price cell has already said so. **The threshold is a setting, so the table says what it currently is** ([§10](10-settings.md)): the footer's first cell names it in days, beside the other things it says about how the table is built.
- The table carries **gross** value and gain — market price, no tax, no fees. This is the counterpart to the Portfolio headline, which is net ([§3.1](03-portfolio.md#31-behaviour)).
- Selecting a row opens the detail panel: **quantity purchased with the number of lots it came from**, **quantity sold**, weighted average cost, invested total including fees, **the annualised return** ([§11.8](11-calculations.md#118-annualised-return)), and then the full hypothetical liquidation breakdown ([§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)) — gross proceeds, the sell fee with the institution whose default it is, taxable gain, tax with the rate and the security it belongs to, net proceeds, and net gain **with its percentage**.
- **Annualised return is the yearly rate the money in this position actually earned**, counting when each purchase was made rather than only how much was put in ([§11.8](11-calculations.md#118-annualised-return)). It sits in the upper half of the panel, among the facts about the position's history, and **not** in the *If sold today* half: it is a statement about what has happened, not about what liquidating would produce. **It is gross of tax**, like everything else on this tab. A one-line explanation sits with it, because the name does not say what it is.
- **The caveat is shown in the panel, next to the number it qualifies**, and it carries three things ([§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)): that average cost is an approximation and a broker may match lots differently, that the tax is worked out for this position alone so a loss on another holding does not reduce it, and that the sell fee is charged once per holding. **The last two both push the figure down**, which is the part worth knowing about an estimate.
- **The footer totals the two money columns** — value and gain — **and states the percentage those two make**: Σ gain ÷ (Σ value − Σ gain), the denominator being what the positions cost, and *undefined* where that is zero ([§11](11-calculations.md)). Quantities, prices and average costs are not totalled: they belong to different instruments. Like the table above it the footer is **gross**, and it is the one place the portfolio's untaxed value is stated as a single figure — the net counterpart is the Portfolio headline ([§3.1](03-portfolio.md#31-behaviour)), and the two are meant to be read against each other. A holding with no price contributes **its cost** to the value and `€ 0,00` to the gain, exactly as its row does, so **neither money total states an omission**: every figure exists, and the ones standing on cost are the ones check 3 is already naming.
- **The footer also carries the portfolio-wide annualised return** ([§11.8](11-calculations.md#118-annualised-return)), and it is the one figure down there that is not a sum of the column above it. It covers **every trade in the file**, positions sold in full included — those being exactly the ones a lifetime performance figure must not drop — against the value of everything still open. **This is the total that does state an omission**: a (security, account) that is **oversold**, or whose security **has no price at all**, is left out whole — every trade in it, not just its value today — and the footer says how many, linking to checks 3, 8 and 9 like the realised-gain totals link to 8 and 9 ([§7.3](#73-sales), [§3.1](03-portfolio.md#31-behaviour), [§11.8](11-calculations.md#118-annualised-return)). **That is the one place this tab treats an unpriced holding differently from the two money columns above**, which carry it at its cost: an *amount* standing on cost is a stated fallback that check 3 already names, while a **rate** computed from it would read as a position that earned exactly nothing — a performance claim nobody measured ([§11.8](11-calculations.md#118-annualised-return)). A merely stale price is not an omission on either. It carries the same one-line explanation as the panel.

## 7.2 Purchases

> **Mockup —** [Purchases tab](../mockups/07-investments.html#purchases)

- Where purchase trades are created, corrected and deleted. **A row is corrected on the form it was recorded on** ([§7.5](#75-recording-a-trade-and-where-securities-come-from)), opened by *Edit* in the row menu with every field filled in with what the row holds; nothing on a row is edited where it sits. *Total cost* and *Matched* are derived and are not on that form, and neither is `kind` — a purchase and a sale are two tabs and a trade never moves between them.
- **The form a trade is corrected on picks its security from the ones already recorded**, rather than searching-or-creating the way the record form does ([§7.5](#75-recording-a-trade-and-where-securities-come-from)): creating a security belongs to the trade that first needs it, and a row being corrected already has one.
- **Account** is the brokerage account the security sits in, written *Institution · Account* like every account column that has no institution column beside it ([§4](04-accounts.md)) — which is what makes the shared institution visible on the row. The cash for the trade moved through a different account — a cash one at the same institution — and pairing the two is what [§11.6](11-calculations.md#116-derived-matching) does.
- **Total cost** is `qty × price + fees`, derived, never entered. It is the figure [§11.6](11-calculations.md#116-derived-matching) pairs against the bank transaction.
- **Matched** shows the date of the bank transaction the application paired the trade with ([§11.6](11-calculations.md#116-derived-matching)). A dash is what check 6 reports.
- Recording a trade **does not** create a transaction.
- Ordering: the same three keys as Transactions — `date`, `insertionSeq`, `id` ([§5.2](05-transactions.md#52-ordering-and-paging)). **Both tabs are shown newest first** — `date DESC, insertionSeq DESC, id DESC` — so the most recent trade is the first row, exactly as that list is shown. **The ascending order of the same three keys is what everything else reads them in**, and it is what [§9](09-checks.md) and [§11.6](11-calculations.md#116-derived-matching) name when they name this ordering, and what the walk of [§11.1](11-calculations.md#111-weighted-average-cost) inserts its one extra key into: the direction is a decision of these two tabs, the keys are the file's. No paging; filters narrow the list instead.
- Filters: security, account — **brokerage accounts only** ([§2](02-domain-model.md)), one at a time or *All*, like every account filter in the application ([§5.3](05-transactions.md#53-filters)) — and period, which is the *from* and *to* pair of [§5.3](05-transactions.md#53-filters), inclusive at both ends.
- **The footer sums the filtered rows**: quantity is not totalled, and fees and total cost are, with the count beside them. It is the counterpart of the Sales footer ([§7.3](#73-sales)) minus the columns Purchases does not have.

## 7.3 Sales

> **Mockup —** [Sales tab](../mockups/07-investments.html#sales)

- The same table as Purchases with **three columns it does not have**. *Taxes* is the capital-gains tax the broker actually withheld, entered from the trade confirmation. *Net proceeds* replaces *Total cost* — `qty × price − taxes − fees`, what actually reached the account. *Realised gain* is derived per [§11.2](11-calculations.md#112-realised-gain-on-a-sale) and is the only figure on either tab that is neither entered nor a restatement of what was.
- **Taxes is zero, not blank, when nothing was withheld** — a sale at a loss, like the March 2020 row in the mockup, or one whose gain the broker offset against a carried-forward loss. The figure feeds check 7.
- **Realised gain is net of everything the broker took**, so a sale can show a gain before tax and a loss after it. It is summed into the *gains and costs* card on Portfolio ([§3](03-portfolio.md)), where it is the one investment number that is a recorded fact rather than an estimate.
- **Realised gain reads *undefined* when the position it came out of has no average cost.** A sale in a (security, account) whose running quantity ever went below zero has no cost basis to measure against: the walk of [§11.1](11-calculations.md#111-weighted-average-cost) stops, no `avgCost` exists, and the figure is undefined rather than zero or blank ([§11.2](11-calculations.md#112-realised-gain-on-a-sale), [§11](11-calculations.md)). The row is shown in full and only that one cell says so. Checks 8 and 9 name the trade that caused it, and putting the dates or the missing purchase right restores the figure ([§9](09-checks.md)).
- The footer sums fees, taxes, net proceeds and realised gain. It is the only place the tax actually paid over ten years is visible as one figure. **The realised-gain total covers the sales that have one** and says how many it left out, which is the same rule the Portfolio *gains and costs* card follows ([§3.1](03-portfolio.md#31-behaviour)); the other three columns are entered figures and always total everything.
- Everything else — ordering, filters, editing, matching, the fact that a sale creates no transaction — is exactly as [§7.2](#72-purchases).

## 7.4 Securities

> **Mockup —** [Securities tab](../mockups/07-investments.html#securities)

- **Everything about a price happens on this tab**: the whole history, the form that records and corrects one, the delete, and *Update prices*. That follows from the model rather than from taste — a Price belongs to a Security ([§2](02-domain-model.md)), so the tab that lists securities is the tab that keeps their prices, and no other screen writes one.
- **One of the two places a security is created**, and the place one is corrected. *Add security* here creates it on its own; the purchase form creates it inline while recording the first trade that needs it ([§7.5](#75-recording-a-trade-and-where-securities-come-from)). The fields are the same either way and so is the record — there is no such thing as a security that came in by one path rather than the other.
- Correcting is the rest of the tab's job: a mistyped ISIN, a renamed instrument, the wrong type, the blended tax rate on a government-bond ETF ([§2](02-domain-model.md)).
- **Exchange is a field here like any other**, a picker over the eighteen of [§2](02-domain-model.md), and it is where a security that comes back with no quote is put right ([§7.6](#76-prices)) — a fetch that finds nothing is most often a `ticker` or an `exchange` that names no listing, and this is the tab that fixes both. Correcting either changes nothing that was ever recorded: the trades and the whole price history belong to the security and stay with it.
- **Ordered by `ticker`, alphabetically**, the same key and the same comparison as Holdings ([§7.1](#71-holdings)). Securities no longer held are in the list like everything else, marked by an em dash in *Held*; nothing sorts them apart.
- **Held** is the quantity across every brokerage account — **closed ones included**, like every other figure in the application ([§11.4](11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth)), since a security sitting in a shut dossier is still held until it is sold — and an em dash when the position is closed. **A security oversold in any account reads an em dash too, in a cell tinted light red**: no holding is derived for that (security, account) at all ([§2](02-domain-model.md)), so there is no quantity to state and none is invented — not for the broken account and not for the others, since a partial sum would read as the position. The tint is what separates it from the ordinary dash: one means nothing is held, the other means nothing can be said. Checks 8 and 9 name the trade ([§9](09-checks.md)), and the cell reads normally again once it is put right.
- **Trades** is what decides whether the security can be deleted; **Prices** does not. Deleting a security deletes its price history with it and the confirmation says how many records that is ([§13](13-validation.md)).
- **The full price history lives here, and every record in it can be corrected or deleted.** *Add price* records one and *Edit* in a row's menu corrects one, and it is **the same form either way**: a value and an as-of date, the date defaulting to today on a new record and opening on its own day on an existing one. Saving a value replaces it; saving another date **moves** the record, replacing whatever occupied the day it lands on ([§2](02-domain-model.md)) — **and neither is confirmed** ([§13](13-validation.md)). **The form says what the chosen day already holds** rather than refusing it, and the day a record already sits on is not counted as a value it would displace. **Deleting is confirmed**, like every delete in the application; **this is the only place a price is deleted**, and it is the only way to undo a price recorded against a date that never had one.
- **Update prices** sits on this tab; [§7.6](#76-prices) is what it does. **It opens a modal and does nothing else**: no setting behind it, and nothing sent by the press itself. The modal is where the securities to ask about are ticked, where how far back is answered, and where what came back is put to the user **before a single record is written** ([§7.6](#76-prices)); the statement of what leaves the machine is on its first page, and the network is reached only from there. It sits beside the list of the securities it lists, which is every one in the file, held or fully sold ([§7.6](#76-prices)). Never press it and the form above is the only way a price gets in, which is a complete way to use the application.
- **The history carries the date, the value and the source** — *manual* or *fetched*, the `source` of [§2](02-domain-model.md), and **this is the only screen that shows it**. It is a label and nothing more: no check, no total and no fetch reads it ([§7.6](#76-prices)). **Any edit a user makes sets it to *manual*** — an edit to the value and an edit to the date alike — so a fetched figure somebody has corrected stops claiming to be the provider's.
- The history is **newest first** — the opposite of every other table in the application.
- Changing a security's **tax rate** changes the [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation) estimate for it and therefore net worth, and changes nothing that was ever recorded — a sale carries the tax the broker actually withheld ([§7.3](#73-sales)).

## 7.5 Recording a trade, and where securities come from

> **Mockup —** [Record a purchase · new security](../mockups/07-investments.html#record-trade)

- **Total cost** is computed and shown live as quantity, price and fees are typed — never entered. It is the figure [§11.6](11-calculations.md#116-derived-matching) pairs against the bank transaction.
- **Account** lists brokerage accounts only and starts empty, no picker in the application remembering what was chosen last ([§5.5](05-transactions.md#55-add-transaction)).
- **A security can be created inline, here.** Typing an ISIN or ticker searches existing securities; if none matches, the form expands with the fields needed to create one, and it is saved together with the trade. No “create a security first” step is *required* — the Securities tab has *Add security* for when it is wanted anyway ([§7.4](#74-securities)).
- **The expanded form carries `exchange` with the rest of them**, and it is the field to fill from the trade confirmation rather than from memory: the exchange the order was filled on is the listing whose price belongs to this position ([§2](02-domain-model.md)). Getting it wrong costs nothing that is recorded — only a quote that does not arrive, corrected on the Securities tab ([§7.4](#74-securities)).
- **Tax rate** is pre-filled from `defaultTaxRate` and only needs touching for a government-bond ETF, whose rate is a blend below the ordinary one ([§2](02-domain-model.md)). It affects the [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation) estimate and nothing that is ever recorded.
- **The same form corrects a trade**, opened by *Edit* in its row menu ([§7.2](#72-purchases)) — with one difference: the security is picked from the ones already recorded rather than searched for, a row being corrected already having one.
- **The Securities tab covers everything afterwards** ([§7.4](#74-securities)): correcting the security, and its whole price history.
- **The sale form adds a Taxes field** below Fees, and shows **net proceeds** — `qty × price − taxes − fees` — live in place of total cost. It defaults to zero and is never pre-filled from the hypothetical rate of [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation).

## 7.6 Prices

> **Mockup —** [Update prices · choosing](../mockups/07-investments.html#fetch-choose) · [while it runs](../mockups/07-investments.html#fetch-running) · [the review](../mockups/07-investments.html#fetch-review) · [nothing to write](../mockups/07-investments.html#fetch-nothing)

- Prices are entered per security with an **as-of date**, on the Securities tab and nowhere else ([§7.4](#74-securities)), and every day is kept as history. Recording a price for a day that already has one replaces that day and leaves the rest of the history alone.
- **A price belongs to the security, not to a holding.** The same instrument held at two institutions is two holdings and one price, so a price recorded once moves both rows. **That is why no holding row is where a price is written** ([§7.1](#71-holdings)): a figure edited on one row and landing on two is a figure whose reach the row cannot state, and the tab that owns the security is the one place where what a price belongs to is unambiguous.
- **If two quotes ever differ enough to matter, they are two listings and belong to two securities**, each with its own ISIN and its own holding — which the model already supports.
- **A security with no price at all values at what its position cost**, everywhere a current figure is shown, and fails check 3 by name while it has an open holding ([§9](09-checks.md)) — a fully sold one has no position left to value and no check to fail. That is true on the historical chart of [§11.5](11-calculations.md#115-net-worth-over-time) as well, where **one fallback covers both** the stretch before a security's first recorded price and the whole life of a security that has none, drawn dashed either way. **The annualised return is the one figure that does not follow**: an unpriced position is left out of it whole, a rate being the one thing that may not rest on cost ([§11.8](11-calculations.md#118-annualised-return)).
- **Online lookup has no setting: it is the *Update prices* button ([§7.4](#74-securities)) and nothing else.** Pressing it is the only thing that ever contacts the network — never automatic, never on load, never in the background.
- **The button opens a modal, and that modal is the whole of the pass**: which securities are asked about, how far back, what came back, and what is written. It has **two pages** — the one that chooses, and the one that fills itself in as the answers arrive and becomes the review when they have all landed. **Nothing is written until the second page is confirmed**, and nothing outside the modal moves while it is open.

### The first page — what to ask for

- **Every security in the file is listed, held or fully sold**, ordered by ticker like every other list of securities ([§7.4](#74-securities)). Each row states **what the file already holds** — the security's most recent price and the day it is for, or that it has never been priced — and **what the pass would ask for it**: the first day of its span, and how many days that is.
- **The securities holding an open position are ticked, and the fully sold ones are listed unticked.** A closed position still has a history worth completing, so it is offered and is one tick away; it is not what a weekly press of the button is for, so it is not ticked by default.
- **Five selectors set the ticks in one click** — *All*, *None*, *Held*, *Never priced* and *Stale* — where **stale means exactly what check 3 means** ([§9](09-checks.md)): a price older than `priceStalenessDays` ([§10](10-settings.md)). They set the selection and are not a state of their own: a tick changed by hand afterwards simply stands.
- **How far back is one question with three answers, and the answer covers every ticked security:**
  - **the latest quote the provider has**, whatever day it belongs to — one figure per security, which is what keeping a file current needs and what a pass costs least;
  - **every day since the file last knew a price** — each security asked from **the day after its most recent Price record**, or from **its first purchase** where it holds no price at all;
  - **every day of the security's whole history** — each security asked from **its first purchase**, whatever prices it already holds. This is what fills a history in at first setup, and what comes back is a series per security rather than a figure.
- **There is no asking for a particular day, today included.** A provider is asked for what it has; on a Sunday, on a holiday and before a close what it has is the previous session's, and a span that named a day the user can be shown they did not receive would state something untrue about the pass.
- **A span ends at today where the position is open, and at the day of the last sale where it is closed.** Nothing the application draws needs a price for a day after a position closed ([§11.5](11-calculations.md#115-net-worth-over-time)), and a closed position that is bought back is asked again from the day the pass is next run.
- **A span never begins after it ends.** A security already priced past the end of its span is asked for that one day, which comes back holding what the file already holds and writes nothing.
- **A security with no purchase at all is asked for the latest quote whichever answer was given**, having no history to build and no first day to begin one from.
- **There is no per-security span, and no answer is remembered from last time**: one question, one answer, every ticked security asked on the same terms. **Nothing about the modal is remembered between two presses** — not the span, not the selection, not a preference and nothing in the file — so it opens the same way every time.
- **The press that starts the pass says what it is about to ask for**, in securities and in days, and it is the only control on the page that reaches the network.

### The second page — while it runs

- **The review table is drawn when the pass starts and fills in row by row**, in the order the securities are asked in, so nothing is redrawn or re-ordered when the last answer lands. A row that has already answered reads exactly as it will when the pass has ended.
- **A pass says how far it has got while it runs.** A whole file asked day by day takes long enough that a still screen reads as a broken one, so the page reports how many securities of how many have been asked about and which one is being asked for now.
- **Nothing is written while it runs**, and what has been gathered so far is not written either.
- **Cancel is the one control that lives while the pass runs.** It **asks first** — what is already on screen is about to be discarded, which the rows do not otherwise say — and then closes the modal. **A cancelled pass ends at nothing**: no record, no half-finished pass, no partial review, every price the file had still standing. **There is no pause and no way to keep what came back**; the remedy for a pass abandoned or failed is to press the button again, or to type the price in by hand ([§7.4](#74-securities)). Nothing about a cancelled or a failed fetch is recorded in the file.
- **Every other control is dead until the last row has resolved**, the ticks included.

### The second page — the review

- **What comes back is put to the user before any of it is written**, and the page accounts for **every security in the file**:
  - **every security that got a quote**, with **the newest value**, **the date that quote belongs to** — the day the quote is *for*, not the day the button was pressed — and **what writing it would do to the file**: how many days came back, **how many of them are days the file holds no price for**, **how many would land on a day holding a different value**, and **how many are days that already hold exactly this figure**;
  - **the securities the provider had no quote for**, named;
  - **the securities that could not be fetched at all**, named, each with its reason;
  - **the securities that were not asked about**, named — the rows left unticked on the first page — so that no security in the file is silently missing from the report;
  - **the provider's own reference date**, where it gives one — the moment its figures are as of, which is not necessarily the date any individual quote carries.
- **A day that came back holding what the file already holds for that day is not written at all**, and its record is not touched — **a value typed by hand and confirmed by the provider stays `manual`** ([§2](02-domain-model.md)). It is counted as unchanged and nothing more. This is what makes **the same pass run twice write nothing the second time**, and it is why *replaces* below means the value differs rather than the day being occupied.
- **A row states its replacements and can show them.** Expanding a row lists **the days that would land on a different value**, each with the day, the value stored against it with its source, and the value that would replace it. **Only those days**: the new days can number thousands and overwrite nothing, and the replacements are the only writes that take something away.
- **The page totals the pass in one line**: how many prices confirming writes and over how many securities, how many of them replace a different value and how many of those were typed by hand, how many days came back unchanged, and how many were dropped.
- **Every row that has something to write is ticked when the pass ends, and any of them can be unticked.** A row with nothing to write, one that failed, one the provider had no quote for and one that was not asked about **cannot be ticked at all**. This is the one thing the review lets the user narrow, and it is worth having: a pass is no longer one homogeneous thing, and a series that came back visibly wrong is a row there is a reason to drop.
- **Confirm and every ticked row is written**, each as a `fetched` Price record dated the day its quote is for, replacing whatever that day already held ([§2](02-domain-model.md)), **a hand-typed value of a different figure included**: a day holds one price and the last word on it wins, and what the history then shows against that day is *fetched* ([§7.4](#74-securities)), which is the record saying so.
- **Cancel and nothing at all is written** — no record, no half-finished pass, every price the file had still standing.
- **Going back to the first page keeps the choice and the selection and discards what came back.** Nothing gathered survives a return: the results are the answer to the question that was asked, and a question being asked again is a new pass.
- **Asking again covers only the securities whose answer could come back different, and it says how many those are.** A request that did not complete is one of them. **A refusal is not** — a quote stated in USD is stated in USD every time, and so are a quote with no currency, one of zero or less, and one dated in the future — and neither is a listing the provider does not carry. **The offer is absent where nothing qualifies.** What it runs is an ordinary pass over those securities with the same span, and its answers land in the same table.
- **A pass with nothing to write still shows its review** — every security having failed is a report worth reading — with nothing that can be ticked, the write refused, and the way back to the first page where it always is.

### What is refused, and what leaves the machine

- **A refusal falls on the security where the provider states it once, and on the day where the day states it.** The currency is stated once for a whole series, so **a series in anything but EUR is refused whole**, its security appearing among the ones that could not be fetched exactly as a single quote does. **A day is another matter.** A series of four thousand days will carry a handful the provider has no figure for — a holiday it lists anyway, a halt, a gap it does not fill — and one of those is not grounds to throw away the other three thousand nine hundred. **A day whose figure is absent, zero or less, or dated in the future is dropped from the series and counted**, the row saying how many were dropped and the rest of the series standing. **A series every day of which was dropped is a security the provider had no quote for**, which is what it amounts to.
- **A quote the provider dates in the future is neither offered nor written**, on the same rule that governs a typed one ([§13](13-validation.md)). **Where it is a security's one quote it appears among the ones that could not be fetched**, with that as its reason; where it is one day of a series it is a dropped day, per the rule above.
- **A quote of zero or less is refused on the same terms.** A price is `> 0` wherever one is typed ([§13](13-validation.md)) and a fetch is not an exception to it. This is worth stating rather than leaving to the general rule, because a zero is the one bad value that would look like ordinary missing data once written: a holding priced at zero reads as *no price at all* on [§7.1](#71-holdings) while **valuing at nothing rather than at its cost** ([§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)), so a provider returning an empty field as a number would quietly take a real position out of net worth — and would do it **silently**, check 3 counting the security as priced, the chart drawing it solid and the annualised return admitting it. A written zero is worse than no price at all, which is the whole reason this is stated rather than left to the general rule. The security appears among the ones that could not be fetched, with that as its reason, and the price it already had still stands — and within a series it is a dropped day, on the same grounds and to the same end.
- **A quote in any currency but EUR is neither offered nor written either, and this is the one place the EUR-only premise has to be defended rather than assumed.** Every amount in the file is EUR and no record carries a currency ([§1](01-premise-and-constraints.md), [§13](13-validation.md)) — which means a number arriving from outside carries no marking to say it is something else, and a dollar quote written into a Price record would go straight into a holding's value, the breakdown, the chart and net worth, wrong by the exchange rate and **invisible to every check in [§9](09-checks.md)**. So: **the currency the provider states is compared against EUR, and anything else is refused**, the security appearing among the ones that could not be fetched with that as its reason. **A provider that does not state a currency at all is the same refusal** — an unlabelled number is not evidence of anything — and it is a requirement on which provider is chosen, stated as such below, rather than a case to be worked around.
- **In v1 that makes a non-EUR instrument one with no quote available**, priced by hand or not at all. It is not an error in the file and nothing else about it changes: the security, its trades and its holding are ordinary, `€` still means what it says because the user typed the figures, and check 3 ages the price as usual. **Currency remains entirely out of scope** ([§15](15-out-of-scope.md)); what this rule adds is not currency support but a closed door where an unmarked number could otherwise have walked in.
- **A security the provider has no quote for is left exactly as it is.** No record is written, the price it already had still stands and still ages towards check 3, and nothing is carried forward or invented. The same is true of one the provider does not recognise, and of a fetch that fails outright.
- **Check 3 measures staleness from the reference dates and not from the moment the button was pressed.**
- **What leaves the machine is one security's listing and the day to start from, and nothing else** — its `ticker` and its `exchange` ([§2](02-domain-model.md)), put into whatever form the provider takes, and the first day of the span where a span is being asked for — and what comes back is a quote — or a series of them, one per day — each with the date it belongs to, under the currency the provider states once. **The day is the one thing a history pass sends that the shorter pass does not**, and it is worth naming rather than passing over: it is the day after a price already recorded, or the day of a first purchase, so it says *that something was bought or priced around then* and nothing whatever about what, how much, at what price or through whom. **The ISIN never leaves**, having no part in the request. **No amounts, quantities, balances or account data ever leave** either, and nothing about the position: not the quantity held, not what it cost, not the account it sits in.
- **The application must state this on the first page of the modal, where the request has not yet been made** — and the first page states it twice over, in a sentence naming the provider and in the column that gives, security by security, the first day each request will carry. **It is stated before rather than after**: the moment a user can still decide is the moment before the button on that page is pressed, and a statement made only once the answers are in explains what has already happened. Nothing is said beside the button on the Securities tab, which asks for nothing and sends nothing.
- **Which provider, and exactly what it is sent, are settled when the application is built, not here.** This section fixes the shape of the request — **a listing and a span out, and back one dated quote per day of it with the currency they are stated in** — one press, and one provider for the whole pass. **A provider that can answer only for today does not meet the requirement**, the span being what a history is built from. What this document commits to is: **one provider, built in, free to use, and named on the page that asks**, with nothing sent beyond a listing. **A provider that cannot state the currency of a quote does not meet the requirement**, since the refusal above depends on it. When the provider has to change, that is a new version.
- **Needing an account and an API key does not disqualify a provider.** Should one ever be chosen that requires a key, **the key is a setting like any other** ([§10](10-settings.md)), the screen names the provider it belongs to and where one is obtained, and the modal says so plainly while no key is set — a fetch that cannot run is a line in the review like any other failure, never a blocked screen. What stays disqualified is **a credential compiled into a public repository**, which configures nothing and merely hides a shared secret in plain sight. **v1 adds no such setting**, its provider requiring none, and the free requirement above is what this rule may not be used to soften: a key that costs money is a provider this application does not use.
- The application is fully usable without ever pressing the button. A provider failure is a line in the review, never a blocked screen.
