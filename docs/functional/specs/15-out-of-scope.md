# §15 — Out of scope for v1

*[Index](../README.md) · [why it is this way](../why/15-out-of-scope.md)*

Each of these was raised and deliberately declined. They are listed so they are not reintroduced by accident.

---

| Not building | What is there instead |
| --- | --- |
| Currency, in any form | Everything is EUR and **nothing records a currency**: no field on accounts or securities, no picker on a form, no symbol in the preferences ([§1](01-premise-and-constraints.md), [§13](13-validation.md)). |
| A second interface language | English only, no language selector. Strings stay separable so it remains possible ([§1](01-premise-and-constraints.md)). |
| Tax systems other than the Italian one | Rate per security is a parameter, not a constant ([§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)). |
| **The parts of the Italian system itself that are not modelled** | **Italian, here, means one rate on one gain under the *regime amministrato***. A sale carries the tax the broker actually withheld ([§2](02-domain-model.md)), which is what that regime does, and check 7 compares net proceeds against what the bank credited ([§9](09-checks.md)). Under the *dichiarativo* regime nothing is withheld: the proceeds arrive gross, `taxes` on the sale is 0, and the tax is settled months later with the annual return — an ordinary payment in *Income & other taxes* ([§6.3](06-categories.md#63-category-list)) on a date of its own, with **nothing tying it to the sale that produced it**. That is a legal way to record it and an unsupported way to read it: the realised gain reads as though no tax were due ([§11.2](11-calculations.md#112-realised-gain-on-a-sale)) and the payment sits in the expense report. The rows below name the rules missing *within* the withheld case — loss offsetting, carried-forward losses, the gross-of-expenses rule — and two more sit outside it. **The pension fund's exit rate is a number typed into a field**, not one derived from years of participation ([§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)). **Wealth taxes are counted only when they are charged**: *imposta di bollo* on an account or a securities deposit arrives as a bank transaction under *Wealth tax* and is never accrued, forecast, or checked against the balance it was levied on. |
| Export in any format | The file is the data, and [§12](12-storage.md) documents it well enough to read with a script. No CSV, no report export. |
| **A password on the file, or encryption of any kind** | **Nothing is asked for and nothing is protected**: the file opens because it is on disk, and whatever can read it can read all of it — the same property that lets a script write it and lets the migration script exist ([§12](12-storage.md)). Protection belongs to the machine: a user account, an encrypted volume, an encrypted vault folder, which [§12](12-storage.md) already says the file may sit inside. Building it would reach past a prompt on open — **every backup is a copy in the same shape**, the pre-upgrade and displaced-external copies included, and each would need the same treatment — and a one-file ledger with no account behind it has **no recovery path at all**: a forgotten password is ten years of history gone, with nobody to appeal to. |
| Accessibility work and keyboard shortcuts | Not designed for v1. Dark theme, mouse-first, one user. |
| Editing the category list at runtime — **adding, renaming or deleting one** | Categories are stored records with stable ids ([§2](02-domain-model.md)), but the application seeds them and offers no editor of any kind: the list of [§6.3](06-categories.md#63-category-list) is what a file has, and **the way it changes is a new version of the application**, whose upgrade says what becomes of the rows and rules pointing at anything it retires ([§12](12-storage.md)). Roles, order and `receiptTracked` are part of that seed and are not the user's either — checks and the report read them ([§9](09-checks.md), [§6.1](06-categories.md#61-report)). |
| Undo / redo | Backups and delete confirmation cover it for now. |
| Bank-specific import profiles | One fixed column order; the user pre-formats the export. Explicitly future work. |
| Bank exports in any other shape | Trailing-sign negatives (`54,80-`), parenthesised negatives, a separate debit/credit column pair, a `D`/`C` marker, a header row, a non-breaking space where a thousands separator is expected, quoted fields and a leading byte-order mark are none of them handled. A **leading `+`** and a **`€` or `EUR` marker** on the amount *are* read, as is an ordinary space used as a thousands separator when the control says so ([§5.7](05-transactions.md#57-bulk-import)). Neither are dates carrying a month name, a weekday, a time or a two-digit year (`11/07/26`). Three columns, fixed order, one signed amount, and the three format controls of [§5.7](05-transactions.md#57-bulk-import) saying what its characters mean: reshape the export before pasting, and anything that does not fit is reported as a row that cannot be read rather than guessed at. A fourth column and beyond is the one thing tolerated. |
| Term-deposit maturity | Not tracked. A term deposit carries no maturity date and nothing announces one — the date goes in the account's notes if it is wanted, and the bank is what tells you the money has moved. |
| Bulk import of trades | The form of [§7.5](07-investments.md#75-recording-a-trade-and-where-securities-come-from) is the one way in. |
| Bulk import of payslips | The ten years of history come in with everything else, through the migration script. |
| A migration importer | A one-off custom script will load the Excel history. |
| Category groups | The list is readable flat. |
| Monthly reports | Yearly only. |
| Voucher expiry | Not tracked. |
| Automatic transaction generation from trades, or paired transfer entry | Both sides are recorded independently ([§1](01-premise-and-constraints.md), [§9](09-checks.md)). |
| A warning tier on checks | Pass or fail only ([§9](09-checks.md)). |
| **Turning a check off** | **All fourteen run on every file, always** ([§9](09-checks.md)), and [§10](10-settings.md) holds nothing that silences one — the preferences a check reads are its thresholds and its windows, never whether it runs. **A check with nothing to examine already costs nothing**: it passes and states its reach, so a file with no pension fund gets a passing check 10 rather than a failure to suppress, and a payslip with three zeroes takes no part in check 5. What a switch would be for is a check that fails and is meant to — which is a reason to fix the file or change the check, not to make the badge and the Portfolio banner mean *nothing is wrong here that I have not agreed to ignore* ([§3.1](03-portfolio.md#31-behaviour)). |
| Mobile or web deployment | Desktop on macOS, Windows and Linux ([§1](01-premise-and-constraints.md)), single machine. |
| **Telling the operating system what a `.spiccioli` file is** | **Spiccioli takes a ledger handed to it and does the right thing with one** ([§12.2](12-storage.md#122-the-menu-bar-and-which-file-is-open)) — opened at launch instead of the launch screen, opened as a departure while another file is open, and answered by nothing but the window coming forward where it is the file already open. **What is missing is the registration**: nothing tells macOS, Windows or Linux that the extension belongs to Spiccioli, so **double-clicking a ledger does not open it** and the file carries the icon of something nothing has claimed. That is packaging work and it is three unrelated pieces of it — a document type declared in the macOS bundle, registry entries written per user on Windows, which the installer Spiccioli uses has no way to write, and a MIME type with a `.desktop` entry on Linux — none of which can be built or tested on one machine. **The ways in that already work are *Open with…* and a path on the command line**, and the application needs nothing further on the day the registration is done. |
| Percentage or tiered sell fees, and **fees charged per order** | Flat per institution, **charged once per holding** ([§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)). Five positions at one broker are estimated with five fees, which understates net worth and does so most on the portfolios it fits worst. It is also what pushes a tiny holding — and occasionally a whole slice of the breakdown ([§3.1](03-portfolio.md#31-behaviour)) — below zero. |
| Setting losses against gains in the liquidation estimate | The tax is worked out one holding at a time and is 0 on a loser ([§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)), so a portfolio of winners and losers is estimated with more tax than it would really pay. **Already correct for the two ETF types**, whose gains cannot be reduced by losses at all, and conservative for `Stock` and `ETC`. §11.3 records why the fix is structural rather than arithmetic. |
| **Debts of any kind** — a mortgage, a loan, a credit card balance | **Assets only**, stated as a premise ([§1](01-premise-and-constraints.md)): there is no account type that holds a debt, so “net worth” is the total of what is owned ([§11.4](11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth)). Correct while there is none, and **a `Liability` account type with a negative balance is most of what lifting it would take** — the arithmetic already sums signed balances over every account. |
| Inflation, and any figure in real terms | Every amount is nominal and figures a decade apart are added as they stand — the *gains and costs* card sums a 2016 bank fee with a 2026 one, and the net worth chart plots ten years in mixed-vintage euros ([§3.1](03-portfolio.md#31-behaviour)). No index is held and none is fetched. |
| A time-weighted return | The annualised return of [§11.8](11-calculations.md#118-annualised-return) is **money-weighted** — what the money earned. The time-weighted counterpart, which removes the effect of when money went in and is what a fund factsheet quotes, needs the portfolio valued on **every** contribution date, and the price history is not that dense. |
| TFR left accruing with the employer | `severanceContribution` is the TFR that reached the **fund** ([§2](02-domain-model.md)). Severance left with the employer accrues monthly, is revalued by statute, and has **no account, no field and no place in net worth**. Nothing warns that it is missing, because nothing in the file could know. |
| **A net-to-gross ratio**, on a payslip or on a year | **Removed rather than fixed.** Nothing anywhere divides a net figure by a gross one ([§11.7](11-calculations.md#117-salary-figures)). The payslip table's derived columns are down to one, `netSalary`, and the per-year table carries totals and hourly rates. |
| **A pension in place of a salary** | [§8](08-salaries.md) models **employment and nothing else** — a contract with months per year and hours per day, payslips carrying a *totale lordo* line, a net payment, a car deduction and three fund credits, and hourly rates over a year's working days ([§11.7](11-calculations.md#117-salary-figures)). **A retirement pension has none of that shape**, and there is no second kind of contract, no second form and no figure on that screen that would mean anything for one. What is there instead: the payment is **an ordinary transaction under *Other income*** ([§6.3](06-categories.md#63-category-list)) — deliberately **not *Salary***, whose role makes check 4 demand a payslip for every such transaction ([§9](09-checks.md)) — so it counts in Net, in the report and in net worth, and Salaries stays about the job that ended. The pension fund is the other half of it: [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation) nets an account that is still accumulating, and **a fund being drawn is a different instrument** — nothing models a lump sum taken, an annuity paid out, or the account emptying as it pays. |
| Recording what a payslip's gross line contains | `gross` stays the *totale lordo* line copied as printed ([§2](02-domain-model.md)), and what an employer puts in it is still unrecorded — but with no ratio built on it, **every figure that reads it reads it the same way** and they stay consistent with each other whatever it contains ([§11.7](11-calculations.md#117-salary-figures)). |
| An opening position on a brokerage account | `openingBalance` is forced to 0 on a `Brokerage` account ([§13](13-validation.md)) and a holding is nothing but the sum of its trades ([§2](02-domain-model.md)), so **a position whose purchase history predates the file has to be entered as a purchase that never happened** — one trade, at the quantity held and a cost worked out from outside the application. Not needed for the file this was written for, which has the whole history. |
| Interest accrued but not yet credited | A term deposit paying at maturity is worth its balance and nothing more until the money lands; a multi-year one therefore reads low for years ([§11.4](11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth)). A `Value adjustment` transaction would express it, and **nothing asks for one** — check 10 prompts a revaluation on pension fund accounts only ([§9](09-checks.md)). |
| Non-EUR quotes from the price provider | **Refused rather than converted** ([§7.6](07-investments.md#76-prices)): a quote whose stated currency is not EUR, or which carries no currency at all, is listed among the securities that could not be fetched. In v1 a non-EUR instrument is simply one with no quote available — priced by hand or not at all — and no rate is fetched, stored or applied anywhere. |
| Splitting a pension fund's opening balance into contributions and revaluation | `openingBalance` counts wholly as contributions ([§2](02-domain-model.md)), so a fund whose history starts mid-life is taxed on an exit base that includes the returns it had already made — understating net worth by the rate times that earlier revaluation ([§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)). **Zero error on a fund recorded from its first credit**, which is the file this was written for. The fix is one field, `openingContributions`, and §11.3 says why it is the cheapest of the known simplifications to remove. |
| Carried-forward capital losses | A loss realised in an earlier year is usable for the four years after it and is worth real money; the file tracks no such balance, and cannot warn that one is about to expire. Sales in the file have plainly used one ([§7.3](07-investments.md#73-sales)). |
| The gross-of-expenses rule on ETF gains | Commissions are deducted from the taxable gain on every type ([§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)); on the two ETF types they may not be deductible. Worth the tax rate applied to one commission — cents on a five-figure position — and **to be confirmed against a real trade confirmation** before anyone acts on it. |
| Marking a same-day round trip | A sale bought back the same day measures its realised gain against an average the later purchase is inside ([§11.2](11-calculations.md#112-realised-gain-on-a-sale)). The ordering rule that causes it is the right one, the case is rare, and no row is marked. |
| A pension fund that credits its three contributions as one payment | Check 5 assumes three separate credits, which is how the fund this document was written around works ([§11.6](11-calculations.md#116-derived-matching)). **How often they arrive is settable and no longer an assumption** ([§10](10-settings.md)); that they arrive as three movements rather than one still is. §11.6 records what is left of the assumption and sketches the shape a future version would use — **not** a return to a single combined figure on the payslip, whose problems are unchanged. |
| **A contribution period that is anything other than one number for the installation** | `pensionContributionMonths` is a preference ([§10](10-settings.md)), so **every pension fund a file holds credits on the same cadence, and has credited on it for the whole history**. True of the file this was written for — one fund, one quarterly cadence, ten years of it — and the whole of what v1 models. **A second fund on a different cadence, or a fund that changed cadence partway through, has no way to say so**: the first wants the setting moved onto the pension fund account and the second wants a date it took effect from, and both are stored fields on a record rather than a preference, so both are a change to the file format and an upgrade with it ([§12](12-storage.md)). Until then, check 5 fails across whichever era does not match the setting, with none of the arithmetic wrong. |
| Sortable columns | Fixed chronological order everywhere; filters narrow instead. |
| Bulk edit of transactions | Bulk *delete* exists ([§5.6](05-transactions.md#56-selecting-and-deleting-in-bulk)); changing many categories at once is what rules are for. |
| Transferring a position between brokers | Not modelled. Recorded as a sale and a purchase, which invents a realised gain and leaves checks 6 and 7 with no bank transaction to pair; both failures are visible. |
| **Individual bonds** — a BTP, a corporate bond, a BOT | **`Bond` is not one of the security types** ([§2](02-domain-model.md)); the four are `Stock`, `Stock ETF`, `Bond ETF` and `ETC`. A **`Bond ETF` is unaffected and is how bond exposure is held** — it is a fund, quoted per unit in euros, with no accrued interest and no maturity, so nothing below applies to it. [*Why it is this way*](../why/15-out-of-scope.md) records what an individual bond would need. |
| Corporate actions — splits, mergers, ISIN changes | Not modelled, and **not workable as an adjusting trade either**. **The v1 answer is to delete every trade in the affected (security, account) and record the position again as it stands after the event** — one purchase, at the quantity now held and the average cost the position actually carries. [*Why it is this way*](../why/15-out-of-scope.md) records what was tried and what each attempt breaks. |
| Attributing dividends and coupons to a holding | They arrive as ordinary transactions under *Interest, dividends & bonuses* and stay there. A holding's gain is therefore price only, never total return — the income is counted, just not against the instrument that produced it. |
| Merging two securities | A mistyped ISIN entered twice produces two securities and two half-holdings. The fix is to correct one, move its trades by editing them, and delete the other ([§7.4](07-investments.md#74-securities)). |
| Showing an oversold position | A (security, account) whose running quantity has **ever** been negative gets no holding row and no derived figures — average cost, invested, gain and the **realised gain on every sale in it** are all undefined ([§11.2](11-calculations.md#112-realised-gain-on-a-sale)) — and that holds whether it is still in deficit or was brought back above zero by a later purchase, since the cost basis does not recover ([§11.1](11-calculations.md#111-weighted-average-cost)). The two totals that would have summed those sales say how many they left out rather than counting them as zero ([§3.1](03-portfolio.md#31-behaviour), [§7.3](07-investments.md#73-sales)). Checks 8 and 9 name the trade instead ([§2](02-domain-model.md), [§9](09-checks.md)). |
| Re-validating a file on open | Validation is for the forms ([§13](13-validation.md)). |
