# §15 — Out of scope for v1

*[Index](../README.md) · no mockups for this section*

Each of these was raised and deliberately declined. They are listed so they are not reintroduced by accident.

---

| Not building | What is there instead |
| --- | --- |
| Currency, in any form | Everything is EUR and **nothing records a currency**: no field on accounts or securities, no picker on a form, no symbol in the preferences ([§1](01-premise-and-constraints.md), [§13](13-validation.md)). |
| A second interface language | English only, no language selector. Strings stay separable so it remains possible ([§1](01-premise-and-constraints.md)). |
| Tax systems other than the Italian one | Rate per security is a parameter, not a constant ([§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)). |
| Export in any format | The file is the data, and [§12](12-storage.md) documents it well enough to read with a script. No CSV, no report export. |
| Accessibility work and keyboard shortcuts | Not designed for v1. Dark theme, mouse-first, one user. |
| Editing the category list at runtime | Categories are stored records with stable ids ([§2](02-domain-model.md)), but the application seeds them and offers no editor. |
| Undo / redo | Backups and delete confirmation cover it for now. |
| Bank-specific import profiles | One fixed column order; the user pre-formats the export. Explicitly future work. |
| Bank exports in any other shape | Trailing-sign negatives (`54,80-`), parenthesised negatives, a separate debit/credit column pair, a `D`/`C` marker, a header row, a non-breaking space where a thousands separator is expected, quoted fields and a leading byte-order mark are none of them handled. A **leading `+`** and a **`€` or `EUR` marker** on the amount *are* read, as is an ordinary space used as a thousands separator when the control says so ([§5.7](05-transactions.md#57-bulk-import)). Neither are dates carrying a month name, a weekday, a time or a two-digit year (`11/07/26`). Three columns, fixed order, one signed amount, and the three format controls of [§5.7](05-transactions.md#57-bulk-import) saying what its characters mean: reshape the export before pasting, and anything that does not fit is reported as a row that cannot be read rather than guessed at. A fourth column and beyond is the one thing tolerated. |
| Term-deposit maturity | Not tracked. A term deposit carries no maturity date and nothing announces one — the date goes in the account's notes if it is wanted, and the bank is what tells you the money has moved. |
| Bulk import of trades | The form of [§7.5](07-investments.md#75-recording-a-trade-and-where-securities-come-from) is the one way in. |
| Bulk import of payslips | The ten years of history come in with everything else, through the migration script. |
| A migration importer | A one-off custom script will load the Excel history. |
| Category groups | The list is readable flat. |
| Monthly reports | Yearly only. |
| Voucher expiry | Not tracked. |
| Automatic transaction generation from trades, or paired transfer entry | Both sides are recorded independently ([§1](01-premise-and-constraints.md), [§9](09-checks.md)). |
| A warning tier on checks | Pass or fail only ([§9](09-checks.md)). |
| Mobile or web deployment | Desktop on macOS, Windows and Linux ([§1](01-premise-and-constraints.md)), single machine. |
| Percentage or tiered sell fees, and **fees charged per order** | Flat per institution, **charged once per holding** ([§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)). Five positions at one broker are estimated with five fees, which understates net worth and does so most on the portfolios it fits worst. It is also what pushes a tiny holding — and occasionally a whole slice of the breakdown ([§3.1](03-portfolio.md#31-behaviour)) — below zero. |
| Setting losses against gains in the liquidation estimate | The tax is worked out one holding at a time and is 0 on a loser ([§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)), so a portfolio of winners and losers is estimated with more tax than it would really pay. **Already correct for the two ETF types**, whose gains cannot be reduced by losses at all, and conservative for the other three. §11.3 records why the fix is structural rather than arithmetic. |
| **Debts of any kind** — a mortgage, a loan, a credit card balance | **Assets only**, stated as a premise ([§1](01-premise-and-constraints.md)): there is no account type that holds a debt, so “net worth” is the total of what is owned ([§11.4](11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth)). Correct while there is none, and **a `Liability` account type with a negative balance is most of what lifting it would take** — the arithmetic already sums signed balances over every account. |
| Inflation, and any figure in real terms | Every amount is nominal and figures a decade apart are added as they stand — the all-time card sums a 2016 bank fee with a 2026 one, and the net worth chart plots ten years in mixed-vintage euros ([§3.1](03-portfolio.md#31-behaviour)). No index is held and none is fetched. |
| A time-weighted return | The annualised return of [§11.8](11-calculations.md#118-annualised-return) is **money-weighted** — what the money earned. The time-weighted counterpart, which removes the effect of when money went in and is what a fund factsheet quotes, needs the portfolio valued on **every** contribution date, and the price history is not that dense. |
| TFR left accruing with the employer | `severanceContribution` is the TFR that reached the **fund** ([§2](02-domain-model.md)). Severance left with the employer accrues monthly, is revalued by statute, and has **no account, no field and no place in net worth**. Nothing warns that it is missing, because nothing in the file could know. |
| **A net-to-gross ratio**, on a payslip or on a year | **Removed rather than fixed.** Nothing anywhere divides a net figure by a gross one ([§11.7](11-calculations.md#117-salary-figures)). The payslip table's derived columns are down to one, `netSalary`, and the per-year table carries totals and hourly rates. |
| Recording what a payslip's gross line contains | `gross` stays the *totale lordo* line copied as printed ([§2](02-domain-model.md)), and what an employer puts in it is still unrecorded — but with no ratio built on it, **every figure that reads it reads it the same way** and they stay consistent with each other whatever it contains ([§11.7](11-calculations.md#117-salary-figures)). |
| An opening position on a brokerage account | `openingBalance` is forced to 0 on a `Brokerage` account ([§13](13-validation.md)) and a holding is nothing but the sum of its trades ([§2](02-domain-model.md)), so **a position whose purchase history predates the file has to be entered as a purchase that never happened** — one trade, at the quantity held and a cost worked out from outside the application. Not needed for the file this was written for, which has the whole history. |
| Interest accrued but not yet credited | A term deposit paying at maturity is worth its balance and nothing more until the money lands; a multi-year one therefore reads low for years ([§11.4](11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth)). A `Value adjustment` transaction would express it, and **nothing asks for one** — check 10 prompts a revaluation on pension fund accounts only ([§9](09-checks.md)). |
| Non-EUR quotes from the price provider | **Refused rather than converted** ([§7.6](07-investments.md#76-prices)): a quote whose stated currency is not EUR, or which carries no currency at all, is listed among the securities that could not be fetched. In v1 a non-EUR instrument is simply one with no quote available — priced by hand or not at all — and no rate is fetched, stored or applied anywhere. |
| Carried-forward capital losses | A loss realised in an earlier year is usable for the four years after it and is worth real money; the file tracks no such balance, and cannot warn that one is about to expire. Sales in the file have plainly used one ([§7.3](07-investments.md#73-sales)). |
| The gross-of-expenses rule on ETF gains | Commissions are deducted from the taxable gain on every type ([§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)); on the two ETF types they may not be deductible. Worth the tax rate applied to one commission — cents on a five-figure position — and **to be confirmed against a real trade confirmation** before anyone acts on it. |
| Marking a same-day round trip | A sale bought back the same day measures its realised gain against an average the later purchase is inside ([§11.2](11-calculations.md#112-realised-gain-on-a-sale)). The ordering rule that causes it is the right one, the case is rare, and no row is marked. |
| A pension fund that credits its three contributions as one payment | Check 5 assumes three separate credits, which is how the fund this document was written around works ([§11.6](11-calculations.md#116-derived-matching)). §11.6 records the assumption and sketches the shape a future version would use — **not** a return to a single combined figure on the payslip, whose problems are unchanged. |
| Sortable columns | Fixed chronological order everywhere; filters narrow instead. |
| Bulk edit of transactions | Bulk *delete* exists ([§5.6](05-transactions.md#56-selecting-and-deleting-in-bulk)); changing many categories at once is what rules are for. |
| Transferring a position between brokers | Not modelled. Recorded as a sale and a purchase, which invents a realised gain and leaves checks 6 and 7 with no bank transaction to pair; both failures are visible. |
| **Individual bonds** — a BTP, a corporate bond, a BOT | **`Bond` is not one of the security types** ([§2](02-domain-model.md)); the four are `Stock`, `Stock ETF`, `Bond ETF` and `ETC`. A **`Bond ETF` is unaffected and is how bond exposure is held** — it is a fund, quoted per unit in euros, with no accrued interest and no maturity, so nothing below applies to it. *Why it is this way* records what an individual bond would need. |
| Corporate actions — splits, mergers, ISIN changes | Not modelled, and **not workable as an adjusting trade either**. **The v1 answer is to delete every trade in the affected (security, account) and record the position again as it stands after the event** — one purchase, at the quantity now held and the average cost the position actually carries. *Why it is this way* below records what was tried and what each attempt breaks. |
| Attributing dividends and coupons to a holding | They arrive as ordinary transactions under *Interest, dividends & bonuses* and stay there. A holding's gain is therefore price only, never total return — the income is counted, just not against the instrument that produced it. |
| Merging two securities | A mistyped ISIN entered twice produces two securities and two half-holdings. The fix is to correct one, move its trades by editing them, and delete the other ([§7.4](07-investments.md#74-securities)). |
| Showing an oversold position | A (security, account) whose running quantity has **ever** been negative gets no holding row and no derived figures — average cost, invested, gain and the **realised gain on every sale in it** are all undefined ([§11.2](11-calculations.md#112-realised-gain-on-a-sale)) — and that holds whether it is still in deficit or was brought back above zero by a later purchase, since the cost basis does not recover ([§11.1](11-calculations.md#111-weighted-average-cost)). The two totals that would have summed those sales say how many they left out rather than counting them as zero ([§3.1](03-portfolio.md#31-behaviour), [§7.3](07-investments.md#73-sales)). Checks 8 and 9 name the trade instead ([§2](02-domain-model.md), [§9](09-checks.md)). |
| Re-validating a file on open | Validation is for the forms ([§13](13-validation.md)). |

---

## Why it is this way

- **Currency** is a version of its own: a second one needs a rate table and a decision at every total about what is being added to what — and a one-valued field stored in advance would not have bought that version anything. **Refusing a non-EUR quote is not a step towards it**: it is the opposite, a door closed so that the premise stays true. Everywhere else the premise holds because nothing could carry another currency; a fetch is the one path by which a number the user did not type reaches an amount, so it is the one place the premise has to be defended rather than assumed ([§7.6](07-investments.md#76-prices)).
- **Debts are absent from the premise rather than from the model**, which is the distinction worth keeping. Nothing in [§11.4](11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth) assumes a balance is positive — it sums signed figures over every account, and an overdrawn one already contributes a negative. What is missing is a type that means *this is owed*, and the reason not to add one now is that a debt has a schedule, an interest rate and a remaining term, none of which an account holds and all of which someone would immediately want.
- **Inflation is declined because a real-terms figure needs a source of truth the file has no way to hold.** It would mean an index, kept up to date from somewhere, and a decision at every figure about which year's euros it is quoted in — including on the net worth chart, where the honest version restates every point and the resulting line is a different shape from the one above it. For a ledger whose job is to say what is there, nominal is the right unit, and the arithmetic of comparing 2016 with 2026 is one the reader can do knowing what the figures are.
- **A time-weighted return would answer a question this file is not for.** It measures how the investments performed independently of when money was put in, which is the right way to judge a fund and the wrong way to judge what happened to a person's savings — and it is the one of the two that the data cannot support, since it needs the whole portfolio valued on every purchase date and the price history is a decade of weekly-ish records with gaps. The money-weighted figure is both the more useful one here and the one that is free ([§11.8](11-calculations.md#118-annualised-return)).
- **TFR with the employer is the largest thing this file can be silently missing**, and it is worth naming for that reason alone. Every other omission here is a refinement of a figure that exists; this one is an asset that does not appear at all, on a screen whose headline claims to be net worth. It is out of scope because recording it properly means a monthly accrual and a statutory revaluation — an account whose balance the application would have to *compute* rather than record, which nothing else here does ([§1](01-premise-and-constraints.md)).
- **The net-to-gross ratio was removed, and the whole difficulty around the gross line went with it.** The figure was a percentage nobody acted on: the wedge between gross and net is set by tax law, it barely moves from month to month, and no decision on any screen turned on it. It was also **the only figure in the application that divided an exactly-defined number by one copied off a document** — `netSalary`, defined to the euro by [§11.7](11-calculations.md#117-salary-figures), over whatever an employer's payroll prints as *totale lordo*. That mismatch was the entire reason the composition of that line mattered, and it mattered nastily: reimbursements inside the line push the ratio down, a car deduction reducing the line pushes it up, so a payslip carrying both yields a perfectly ordinary-looking number that is wrong at both ends, with no check able to see it.
- **What is left is a much smaller thing, and it needs no test and no correction.** `Σ gross`, `yearAvgGross` and `grossPerHour` are a sum, a mean and a rate over one field, so they are consistent with each other and comparable across years whatever the line contains. The residue is that they are not comparable with somebody else's idea of gross pay — a level effect, visible, and nothing like an error that cancels itself.
- **Deleting the figure was cheaper than defining it**, which is the general lesson worth keeping: the two-payslip test, the recorded alternative formula and the note about which convention an employer uses were all machinery in service of a number nobody was going to read. Detecting the convention automatically was never on the table anyway — the only evidence is `gross` against `contractGross` across months, and bonuses, overtime and part-months move that difference for reasons that have nothing to do with the question.
- **A fabricated opening trade reaches much further than it looks**, which is why it is recorded even though the file this was built for needs none. That single invented purchase sets `avgCost`, and through it the invested total, the gain, the hypothetical tax and **the realised gain on every later sale in that position** ([§11.1](11-calculations.md#111-weighted-average-cost), [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)); its date decides where the net worth chart begins, the line starting at the earliest of any opening date, transaction or trade ([§11.5](11-calculations.md#115-net-worth-over-time)); and it has no bank transaction behind it, so **check 6 reports it for as long as the file exists** ([§9](09-checks.md)). **Nothing marks it and no check can name it as invented** — it is a purchase like any other, which is exactly the problem. What would fix it is an opening position on a holding: a quantity, an average cost and a date, recorded as a statement about where the history starts rather than as a trade that happened, which keeps it out of check 6 and out of realised-gain arithmetic. It is the same shape `openingBalance` already has for a cash account, and it is a version's work.
- **Accrued interest is left out because the alternative is a figure that ages.** A `Value adjustment` on a term deposit is already legal and already counts ([§6.3](06-categories.md#63-category-list)); what is declined is anything that computes or prompts for one, since a prompt would need the rate, the compounding and the maturity date, and the last of those is deliberately not tracked either. Under-reading a term deposit until it pays is a known and bounded error in the safe direction.
- **Another tax system** is a change to [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation) alone, since the rate is already a parameter on the instrument; anything beyond that is future work.
- **Individual bonds: why the type is absent rather than merely unsupported, and what adding it would take.** A `Bond` type that existed and half worked would be worse than no type at all, because the file would hold a wrong cost basis and two checks would fail forever with nothing on screen saying why. There are three problems and the second has no workaround.
  - **A bond is quoted as a percentage of face value, not in euros per unit.** You hold *€ 10.000 nominal* of a BTP and the screen says `98,35`, meaning 98,35% of face — € 9.835. The trade record computes `quantity × unitPrice`, which gives € 983.500. Entering the price as `0,9835` gets the total right and leaves the price field holding a figure no statement or website prints, with only two decimals of the real quote left inside `decimal(4)`; entering the quantity as `10` lots of € 1.000 is wrong in the other direction. **Neither survives *Update prices***, which writes the provider's `98,35` straight into a Price record and therefore into net worth, out by a factor of a hundred, silently ([§7.6](07-investments.md#76-prices)).
  - **The cash that moves is not the price.** A bond pays its coupon once or twice a year, so a buyer part-way through a coupon period repays the seller the interest accrued so far — the buyer will collect the whole coupon later. Buying € 10.000 nominal at 98,35 six months into a 3% year costs **€ 9.835 of bond plus about € 150 of accrued interest**, and € 9.985 leaves the account. `total = qty × price + fees` cannot express that, so **check 6 fails on every bond purchase in the file, permanently**, and check 7 on every sale. There is no way to record around it: folding the accrued interest into the price corrupts the cost basis and the price history, and recording it as a separate transaction breaks the one-trade-one-transaction pairing that checks 6 and 7 are ([§11.6](11-calculations.md#116-derived-matching)).
  - **A bond ends.** At maturity the issuer repays face value and the position is gone. There is no redemption event; the nearest thing is a sale at exactly 100, which works but is prompted by nothing — a matured bond sits in the portfolio until check 3 notices the price has gone stale.
  - **And not every bond is the same shape.** A BOT or another zero-coupon has no accrued interest at all, but its whole return is taxed as *interest* rather than as a capital gain, which is a different calculation again and not the one [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation) performs.
  - **What it would take**, for whoever picks this up: a per-100 rule on the trade total, the cost basis, the liquidation estimate and the chart, driven off the type; a price column and price editor that mean *per 100 nominal* on some rows and *per unit* on others; and an `accruedInterest` field on the trade that goes **into the cash total and stays out of the cost basis**, because it is a prepaid coupon and not part of what the bond cost. That last rule is the delicate one — it is wrong quietly rather than loudly — and it is the reason this is a version's work rather than an afternoon's.
  - **Dropping the type costs nothing today** and is reversible: no file holds a `Bond`, and adding the type back is additive rather than a migration.
- **The four tax simplifications are declined together, and for one reason.** Loss offsetting, carried-forward losses, the gross-of-expenses rule and the flat sell fee are all refinements to a figure that is **already labelled an estimate on every screen it appears on** ([§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)). Two of them err towards understating net worth, one is worth cents, and one is already right for the instruments most of the money is in. What they have in common is that the cheap-looking ones are not cheap: offsetting and a per-order fee both turn the tax from something a holding has into something the portfolio has, which then has to be handed back to individual holdings so that account balances and the breakdown by type still add up ([§11.4](11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth), [§3.1](03-portfolio.md#31-behaviour)). **The estimate is allowed to be an estimate**; what is not allowed is for it to be wrong in a way nothing on the screen admits, which is why each of them is written down with the direction it errs in.
- **Carried-forward losses are the one of the four worth building first**, if any of them ever is, because it is the only one that is not a refinement of an existing figure: it is an asset the file does not know about, with an expiry date the file cannot warn about, and a value the user could act on.
- **A same-day round trip is not marked** because the marker would appear on a correct figure far more often than on a distorted one — the ordering rule exists precisely so that the ordinary same-day buy-and-sell is an ordinary round trip — and a caveat that mostly fires on nothing teaches people to ignore it.
- **The pension fund assumption is recorded rather than parameterised** because a setting saying which way a fund credits would be a control the user has to get right to make a check work, on a question they can answer only by watching the check fail. The recorded sketch degrades to the present behaviour on its own, which is the property that makes it worth writing down and not worth building yet.
- **Bulk import of trades** is declined because pasting is for bank exports, which arrive by the hundred every month ([§5.7](05-transactions.md#57-bulk-import)). Trades arrive a few dozen a year, one confirmation at a time, and each one carries a security that may not exist yet — the paste would need a column for that too, and a way to say what to do when it does not match.
- **Bulk import of payslips** is the same reasoning, more so: a dozen a year, ten fields each, and no export to paste from — a payslip is read off a PDF by eye whatever the application offers.
- **Category groups** would add a concept for no gain.
- **Automatic generation between trades and transactions** is refused because the independence of the two sources is what makes the checks meaningful.
- **A warning tier** would become a place for things to sit unfixed.
- **Bulk edit** is refused because rules do the same job repeatably and leave a reason behind.
- **Transferring a position between brokers** is not modelled, and the two failing checks are the price of not building it.
- **Corporate actions: what was tried, what each attempt breaks, and why deleting the position is the answer.** This is worth recording in full, because “enter it as an adjusting trade” is the obvious idea, it was the earlier answer here, and **it does not work**.
  - **A forward split cannot be entered at all.** A 2-for-1 split wants a purchase of the extra shares at a price of nothing: quantity doubles, `costBasis` is untouched, `avgCost` halves, which is exactly right ([§11.1](11-calculations.md#111-weighted-average-cost)). But `unitPrice` must be **greater than zero** ([§13](13-validation.md)), so the trade the arithmetic wants is the one trade the form refuses. Entering `0,0001` instead is not a workaround: it puts a small error into the cost basis of a position that is then wrong for as long as it is held, and silently.
  - **A reverse split is worse, and has no workaround.** It wants shares taken *away*, and the only record that removes shares is a sale — which does `costBasis −= q × avgCost` ([§11.1](11-calculations.md#111-weighted-average-cost)). A 1-for-10 reverse split entered that way destroys nine tenths of the cost basis, so the position looks as though it cost a tenth of what it did, and every figure derived from it is wrong in the flattering direction. It also books a **realised loss of nearly the whole position** ([§11.2](11-calculations.md#112-realised-gain-on-a-sale)), which is a fact about nothing that happened and lands in the all-time card on Portfolio ([§3.1](03-portfolio.md#31-behaviour)), the one figure on that screen that is supposed to be a record rather than an estimate.
  - **Selling everything and buying it back has the same defect in a more expensive form**: it invents a realised gain for the whole position, and it leaves checks 6 and 7 with two trades and no bank transactions to pair them against ([§9](09-checks.md)).
  - **So the position is deleted and re-entered.** The realised gain on the sales that are deleted goes with them, which is the cost and is stated here rather than discovered later: a position that had sales before the event loses their contribution to the all-time figure. Everything after the event is then ordinary. It is a handful of edits, for something that happens to one holding every few years, and it is the only route that leaves no wrong number behind.
  - **Relaxing `unitPrice > 0` was considered and declined.** It would make a forward split enterable and would still leave a reverse split with no representation, so it buys half a case at the cost of a validation rule that currently means something simple and true — a trade happened at a price. The whole feature is one version's work when it is wanted: an adjustment record that scales quantity and leaves the basis alone is what a split actually is, and it is not a trade.
- **Merging two securities** is a handful of edits for something that happens once.
- **An oversold position is not shown** because there is no reading of its figures that would be true, and checks 8 and 9 are the report the situation actually calls for.
- **A file is not re-validated on open** because the migration script is written once, for one file, by the one person who will run it.
- **The import stays strict about shape, and infers nothing at all.** What a row's characters mean is said by three controls the user can see and move ([§5.7](05-transactions.md#57-bulk-import)), and anything that does not fit what they say is reported as a row that cannot be read rather than guessed at. The two decorations that *are* read — a leading `+` and a `€`/`EUR` marker — are the two that cannot be mistaken for anything else, and a fourth column is tolerated because ignoring it cannot misread anything.
