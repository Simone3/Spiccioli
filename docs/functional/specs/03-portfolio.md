## 3.1 Behaviour

- **Failing-check banner.** Shown whenever any check fails, above everything else, with the count of failing checks and linking to [§9](09-checks.md). Absent when all pass.
- **Net worth is the headline** and is what the portfolio is worth in hand ([§11.4](11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth)): ordinary cash at its balance, holdings at their latest known price *less* the hypothetical capital-gains tax and sell fee of [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation), and pension funds *less* the hypothetical exit tax of the same section.
- **Five figures, in this order, and the lower four add up to the headline.** *Net worth* is the headline. The four beneath it are the decomposition of [§11.4](11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth):
  - **Cash** — Σ balances of every cash account that is not a pension fund. Money that is already money, at its face balance: no haircut, no cost basis and no rate anywhere in it.
  - **Securities at cost** — Σ holdings at what was paid for them, purchase fees included ([§11.1](11-calculations.md#111-weighted-average-cost)). What the ledger recorded going in, which is not what the positions are worth now.
  - **Unrealised net investment gain** — what selling every holding today would add to the portfolio or take out of it, net of the tax and the fees of [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation).
  - **Pension funds, net** — Σ balances of the pension fund accounts, after the exit tax of [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation). Contributions and the fund's own revaluation together, in one figure.
- `cash + securities at cost + unrealised net investment gain + pension funds, net = net worth`, **by construction**: the four cover every account exactly once ([§11.4](11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth)). The middle two are one brokerage account seen twice over — its balance is the sum of them — which is why they sit adjacent.
- **That is the only decomposition offered**, and it splits the portfolio by **what kind of claim each figure is**: money held, money put in, what the securities have made on it, and what the pension would leave after its tax. **Read down, the estimation accumulates** — the first two lines are what the ledger recorded, the last two are the two places [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation) is applied. **Neither of the first two is a sum of transactions**, and neither may be labelled as one: cash includes opening balances, which are not transactions, and securities at cost values holdings at their cost basis, which is not a transaction either.
- **A pension fund is one line rather than two**, though its contributions and its revaluation are both derived ([§2](02-domain-model.md)): the exit tax falls on the contributions and not on the revaluation, so there is no honest way to put part of it on a line called *gain* ([§11.4](11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth)). **It is the one asset that is not split into what went in and what it made**, and the reason is the tax rather than the data.
- **The headline figure is an estimate**, and **the note that says so is on the four lines beneath it rather than on the figure itself**. Each of the four carries its own note on hover, and the four together are the [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation) caveat split along the lines it actually applies to. **The two that carry estimation open by saying so and the two that do not say only what their figure is**, which is what makes *read down and the estimation accumulates* something the screen shows rather than something this document asserts. **A note says what a reader needs while looking at the figure and then stops**: what is true of a figure but not needed to read it belongs here and not on the screen. The note's own words are in italics below, and what follows them in roman is this document's and is not on the screen:
  - **Cash** — *Every cash account that is not a pension fund, at its opening balance plus every transaction on it.* No haircut, no cost basis and no rate. **Opening balances are in it, and an opening balance is not a transaction**, which is a constraint on what the line may be called rather than on what its note says.
  - **Securities at cost** — *Every holding still open, valued at what was paid for it with the purchase fees included* ([§11.1](11-calculations.md#111-weighted-average-cost)). Not what the positions are worth now, and **not a sum of transactions**: a holding is carried at its cost basis, which no single row holds.
  - **Unrealised net investment gain** — *An estimate. What selling every holding today would add to the portfolio or take out of it, after the capital-gains tax and the sell fee. The tax is worked out one holding at a time, so a loss on one does not reduce the tax on another, and the fee is charged once per holding — both push this figure down. The gross picture is on Investments.* The estimate is therefore a cautious one rather than an optimistic one, and the gross, untaxed per-holding breakdown is one screen away ([§7.1](07-investments.md#71-holdings)).
  - **Pension funds, net** — *An estimate. Every pension fund at its balance less the exit tax, which falls on what was paid in rather than on what the fund earned, at a rate typed by hand. Netting a fund for tax does not make it liquid: this is still money that cannot be had before retirement.* **It is the one note that says what the haircut does not do**, a fund netted for tax being no more spendable than it was.
- **Every euro of estimation in the headline is on the last two lines**, and both move if a security's `taxRate`, a fund's `exitTaxRate` or an institution's sell fee changes ([§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)). The headline is their sum and has nothing to say that they do not.
- **Gains and costs, all time.** What the portfolio has gained and lost by being kept in banks and funds instead of as cash: **five lifetime figures and their total**. Four are the lifetime sums of the categories carrying the roles `bank fees`, `wealth tax`, `interest and dividends` and `value adjustment` ([§6.3](06-categories.md#63-category-list)); the fifth is realised gain from sales ([§11.2](11-calculations.md#112-realised-gain-on-a-sale)). **Nothing on this card is an estimate** — every line is a recorded fact, which is the whole of what it has and the card above it has not.
- **It reads in one order: what was taken, then what was added, then the total** — bank fees, wealth tax, interest and dividends, value adjustments, realised gain on sales, total. **The two negative lines lead** because the costs of being banked are the part nobody keeps a running figure for.
- **Value adjustments are mostly the pension fund's own revaluation**, and in a file with a decade of history they are the largest line on the card ([§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)). The role is legal on any cash account ([§6.3](06-categories.md#63-category-list)), so a term deposit's accrued interest lands here as well; **the line sums whatever carries the role and does not ask which account it was on.**
- **The total is the sum of the five and is the figure the card exists to produce.** It is the net effect of a decade of being banked and invested, against the alternative of having held the money as cash. **It is an amount and not a rate**, it is not net worth and it does not reconcile to anything else on the screen — the money it is measured against was never held ([§11.8](11-calculations.md#118-annualised-return) is where *how well* is answered, and it covers the investments only).
- **The exit tax and the unrealised gain are not on this card**, though both are money the institutions have made or will take. Both are [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation) estimates and both are on the card above; either one here would make the total half record and half projection.
- **Realised gain covers the sales that have one.** A sale in a (security, account) whose running quantity ever went below zero has no average cost to be measured against and therefore no realised gain at all ([§11.2](11-calculations.md#112-realised-gain-on-a-sale)); those sales are left out of the sum, and the card says how many it left out and links to checks 8 and 9, which name the trades ([§9](09-checks.md)). **The total carries the same omission and the same count**, being made of a line that omits them ([§11](11-calculations.md#11--calculations)).
- **Every figure on this screen counts closed accounts**, the lifetime figures and net worth alike ([§11.4](11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth), [§6.1](06-categories.md#61-report)) — a closing date marks an account and sorts it last, it does not remove it from the arithmetic.
- **Breakdown by type.** Cash accounts contribute under the account's type, **a pension fund at its net balance like everywhere else** ([§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)); holdings contribute under their *security's* type, at the same net value used for net worth. A brokerage account never appears as a slice of its own — it is entirely its holdings — so the types available are the seven cash types plus the four security types ([§2](02-domain-model.md)). Closed accounts and their holdings are in it, like everywhere else, which is what keeps the slices adding to net worth. **Only types actually present get a slice**: eleven is the ceiling, and the portfolio in the mockup happens to hold all eleven. A file with no meal vouchers and no gold would draw nine.
- **The pie** carries one slice per type present, sized by its share, with the slice count at its centre and the same colour keying the list beside it. The list beside it carries the amounts and percentages, **in two columns rather than one** — eleven types down a single column leave the card half empty and the pie stranded beside a stripe.
- **Pointing at a slice names it, and pointing at a row finds its slice.** The centre of the ring reads that type's share and its name in place of the slice count, and the row it belongs to lights up. Eleven slices carry eleven colours and a thin one is quicker to point at than to match by eye. **It states what is already on the screen and hides none of it**: every amount and every share is in the list whether anything is being pointed at or not, so this is a convenience and never the only way to read a figure. A row with no slice is never found this way, there being nothing in the ring to find it with.
- **A type can total less than nothing, and then the amount is shown and the share is not.** An overdrawn current account does it, and so does a holding worth less than the fee it would cost to sell ([§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)). The row keeps its place in the list and shows the negative amount, but reads **0,0%** and is **given no slice**.
- **A type totalling exactly nothing is the same case**: it shows `€ 0,00` and **0,0%** and is given no slice. It is still a row — an emptied voucher balance or a term deposit that has just been paid out is a type the portfolio has and a shape it no longer contributes to.
- **Shares are computed against the total of the positive types**, not against net worth. The two figures differ by exactly the negative amounts, which are on screen a line away. **If no type is positive at all** the pie is replaced by a line saying the portfolio has no positive value to divide up, and the list still shows every amount.
- **Both are ordered by share, largest first**, and in the same order — the list reads in the order the slices are drawn clockwise, across its two columns and then down. Types with no share sort last, **by amount descending** — zero first, then the negatives from least to most. This is the one table in the application ordered by its own amounts (compare [§6.1](06-categories.md#61-report)).
- **Net worth over time**, monthly points from the start of the file to today, plus one at today ([§11.5](11-calculations.md#115-net-worth-over-time)). **It plots the same figure as the headline** — net of the hypothetical tax and sell fee, at each date rather than only at today's — so its last point is the headline, to the cent.
- Any month in which at least one holding had to be valued at cost is drawn dashed, and **pointing at a month names the state its figure is in** — *at least one holding at cost — no price yet* against *holdings, if any, at their latest known price*. The line goes solid at the first month in which every holding had a price. **Only a month earlier than a security's first recorded price can be dashed** — a security with no price at all is worth zero on this line exactly as it is on the card above, which is what leaves the final point equal to the headline to the cent and means today's point is never dashed ([§11.5](11-calculations.md#115-net-worth-over-time)).
- **The two states are named where they apply and not in a key under the chart.** They are one line in two states rather than two measurements, and a key would name two lines where the reader sees one — over the whole chart, rather than over the months each state actually covers. **One note does stay under the chart**, because it is true of every point and so has nowhere else to go: the rate and the fee applied across the whole line are today's, those being the only ones the file records ([§11.5](11-calculations.md#115-net-worth-over-time)). The dashed stretch is the only part that is not net worth as computed everywhere else.
- **Breakdown by account** lists every account with its computed balance — **brokerage and pension fund accounts at the net figures of [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)**, like everywhere else — **in the order of [§4.1](04-accounts.md#41-accounts)**: institution name, then opening date, then account name, with accounts belonging to no institution first and closed ones dimmed and last. Its total is the headline net worth. This and the Accounts screen are the two places every account is shown together, and neither filters ([§2](02-domain-model.md)). Read-only — accounts are created, edited and closed on the Accounts screen ([§4.1](04-accounts.md#41-accounts)).
- **Four columns and no more: institution, account, type and balance.** The institution having a column of its own makes this one of the three places an account is *not* written *Institution · Account* ([§4](04-accounts.md)): the account column names the account alone, and a `Cash` account shows an em dash beside it, exactly as on the Accounts table ([§4.1](04-accounts.md#41-accounts)). The account's notes are on the Accounts screen and nowhere else, which is the exact counterpart of that screen carrying **no balance column** ([§4.1](04-accounts.md#41-accounts)): each of the two shows the stored record or the computed figure, and neither shows both.
- **The Balance column carries a note on hover saying how the figure is arrived at** ([§11.4](11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth)): *Opening balance plus every transaction on standard accounts. A pension fund is shown after its exit tax. A brokerage account reads as the net value of its holdings, after the capital-gains tax and the sell fee.* It is the one column on the screen whose figure means three different things depending on the row it is in, and the note is the three of them and nothing else. **Closed accounts are computed like any other**, which is the reason the column totals to the headline; the note does not say so, that being true of every figure on the screen and stated once here rather than four times over ([§11.4](11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth)).
