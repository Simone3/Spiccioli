## 3.1 Behaviour

- **Failing-check banner.** Shown whenever any check fails, above everything else, naming each failing check in one line and linking to [§9](09-checks.md). Absent when all pass.
- **Net worth is the headline** and is what the portfolio is worth in hand ([§11.4](11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth)): ordinary cash at its balance, holdings at their latest known price *less* the hypothetical capital-gains tax and sell fee of [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation), and pension funds *less* the hypothetical exit tax of the same section.
- **Four figures, in this order, and the lower three add up to the headline.** *Net worth* is the headline. The three beneath it are the decomposition of [§11.4](11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth):
  - **Cash and securities at cost** — Σ balances of every cash account that is not a pension fund, plus Σ holdings at what was paid for them. The part of the portfolio the ledger recorded rather than estimated: opening balances, transactions and purchase prices.
  - **Unrealised net investment gain** — what selling every holding today would add to the portfolio or take out of it, net of the tax and the fees of [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation).
  - **Pension funds, net** — Σ balances of the pension fund accounts, after the exit tax of [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation). Contributions and the fund's own revaluation together, in one figure.
- `cash and securities at cost + unrealised net investment gain + pension funds, net = net worth`, **by construction**: the three cover every account exactly once ([§11.4](11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth)).
- **That is the only decomposition offered**, and it splits the portfolio by **how much of each part is measured and how much is estimated** — recorded, estimated on securities, estimated on the pension. Cash and holdings are not shown as two totals here. **The first line is not a sum of transactions**, and must not be labelled as one: it includes opening balances, which are not transactions, and it values holdings at their cost basis, which is not a transaction either.
- **A pension fund is one line rather than two**, though its contributions and its revaluation are both derived ([§2](02-domain-model.md)): the exit tax falls on the contributions and not on the revaluation, so there is no honest way to put part of it on a line called *gain* ([§11.4](11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth)).
- **The headline figure is an estimate**, and carries the [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation) caveat on hover: it rests on average-cost lot matching, on a pension exit rate typed by hand, and it moves if a security's `taxRate`, a fund's `exitTaxRate` or an institution's sell fee changes. **The caveat says two more things, both of which are about which way the figure leans** ([§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)): the tax is worked out one holding at a time, so a loss on one position does not reduce the tax on another, and the sell fee is charged once per holding — **both of which push the figure down**, so the estimate is a cautious one rather than an optimistic one. **And it says what the haircut does not do**: a pension fund netted for tax is still money that cannot be had before retirement. The gross, untaxed picture of the securities is one screen away, on Investments ([§7.1](07-investments.md#71-holdings)), where the per-holding breakdown lives.
- **All-time card.** Lifetime sums of the categories carrying the roles `bank fees`, `wealth tax` and `interest and dividends`, plus realised gain from sales ([§11.2](11-calculations.md#112-realised-gain-on-a-sale)). Unlike net worth, realised gain is a recorded fact, not an estimate.
- **Realised gain covers the sales that have one.** A sale in a (security, account) whose running quantity ever went below zero has no average cost to be measured against and therefore no realised gain at all ([§11.2](11-calculations.md#112-realised-gain-on-a-sale)); those sales are left out of the sum, and the card says how many it left out and links to checks 8 and 9, which name the trades ([§9](09-checks.md)).
- **Every figure on this screen counts closed accounts**, all-time and net worth alike ([§11.4](11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth), [§6.1](06-categories.md#61-report)) — a closing date marks an account and sorts it last, it does not remove it from the arithmetic.
- **Breakdown by type.** Cash accounts contribute under the account's type, **a pension fund at its net balance like everywhere else** ([§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)); holdings contribute under their *security's* type, at the same net value used for net worth. A brokerage account never appears as a slice of its own — it is entirely its holdings — so the types available are the five cash types plus the four security types ([§2](02-domain-model.md)). Closed accounts and their holdings are in it, like everywhere else, which is what keeps the slices adding to net worth. **Only types actually present get a slice**: nine is the ceiling, and the portfolio in the mockup happens to hold all nine. A file with no meal vouchers and no gold would draw seven.
- **The pie** carries one slice per type present, sized by its share, with the slice count at its centre and the same colour keying the list beside it. The list beside it carries the amounts and percentages.
- **A type can total less than nothing, and then the amount is shown and the share is not.** An overdrawn current account does it, and so does a holding worth less than the fee it would cost to sell ([§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)). The row keeps its place in the list and shows the negative amount, but reads **0,0%** and is **given no slice**.
- **A type totalling exactly nothing is the same case**: it shows `€ 0,00` and **0,0%** and is given no slice. It is still a row — an emptied voucher balance or a term deposit that has just been paid out is a type the portfolio has and a shape it no longer contributes to.
- **Shares are computed against the total of the positive types**, not against net worth. The two figures differ by exactly the negative amounts, which are on screen a line away. **If no type is positive at all** the pie is replaced by a line saying the portfolio has no positive value to divide up, and the list still shows every amount.
- **Both are ordered by share, largest first**, and in the same order — the list reads down in the order the slices are drawn clockwise. Types with no share sort last, **by amount descending** — zero first, then the negatives from least to most. This is the one table in the application ordered by its own amounts (compare [§6.1](06-categories.md#61-report)).
- **Net worth over time**, monthly points from the start of the file to today, plus one at today ([§11.5](11-calculations.md#115-net-worth-over-time)). **It plots the same figure as the headline** — net of the hypothetical tax and sell fee, at each date rather than only at today's — so its last point is the headline, to the cent.
- Any month in which at least one holding had to be valued at cost is drawn dashed and labelled as cost-based; the line goes solid at the first month in which every holding had a price. **Only a month earlier than a security's first recorded price can be dashed** — a security with no price at all is worth zero on this line exactly as it is on the card above, which is what leaves the final point equal to the headline to the cent and means today's point is never dashed ([§11.5](11-calculations.md#115-net-worth-over-time)). The dashed stretch is the only part that is not net worth as computed everywhere else, and the legend also says that the rate and the fee applied across the whole line are today's, those being the only ones the file records ([§11.5](11-calculations.md#115-net-worth-over-time)).
- **Accounts table** lists every account with its computed balance — **brokerage and pension fund accounts at the net figures of [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)**, like everywhere else — and closed ones dimmed and last ([§4.1](04-accounts.md#41-accounts)); its total is the headline net worth. This and the Accounts screen are the two places every account is shown together, and neither filters ([§2](02-domain-model.md)). Read-only — accounts are created, edited and closed on the Accounts screen ([§4.1](04-accounts.md#41-accounts)).

## Why it is this way

- **The decomposition splits net worth by what is measured rather than by where the money sits.** A split by kind of asset is the breakdown further down the same screen, and one figure appearing twice on one screen is a figure that can disagree with itself.
- **The pension fund is a line of its own because the two-line version was telling a lie about the other two.** With pension funds folded into the cash side, a decade of fund revaluations — recorded, real, and on the largest single position in most files — sat inside a figure labelled *at cost*, while the line labelled *unrealised gain* covered only the brokerage and quietly claimed to cover everything. Three lines put every euro of estimation where it actually is: the first line is what the ledger recorded, the second is what the securities estimate, the third is what the pension estimates. It is also the honest place to put an asset that is illiquid in a way neither of the others is.
- **The headline is a liquidation figure and now says so consistently.** Netting the securities and not the pension made it neither market value nor liquidation value — an asset was being counted at face precisely because it was the one that could not be sold. It is still an approximation over sources that behave very differently, and the caveat on the headline is what carries that; what it is not any more is an approximation with one arbitrary exemption in it.
- **The realised-gain total states its omissions** because a total that silently omitted the sales with no cost basis would be the one figure on that card that is not what it says it is.
- **Closed accounts stay in every figure** because a closed account should be an empty one — check 11 fails by name while it is not ([§9](09-checks.md)) — so in a file whose checks pass it contributes nothing to net worth anyway, and in a file whose checks do not it contributes the balance that is really still there. The bank charged those fees, that interest was received, that sale really was made, and money left in a shut account is money the file still says you have.
- **The three roles exist for this card and nothing else.** The category list is fixed ([§6.3](06-categories.md#63-category-list)), so the card could have named its three categories directly and been correct forever — but every other part of the application that cares about a particular kind of money keys off `role`, and one screen quietly matching on names would have been the exception that made the rule untrue. Roles cost nothing and keep the promise whole.
- **A negative or zero type keeps its amount and loses its slice** because a negative arc cannot be drawn, and pretending otherwise would leave a circle whose parts sum to more than the circle; a slice of no size is not a slice. The amount is the whole reason anyone would want the row, and a row saying so is worth more than a row that vanished.
- **Shares are computed against the positive types** so the column keeps adding to 100% while a negative type sits in it.
- **The pie and the list share one order** so that finding a slice in the legend is following a line rather than searching, and the whole column reads downwards from the largest contribution to the deepest hole. The pie exists to make the shape of the portfolio readable at a glance. This is the one table sorted by its own amounts because there are nine rows, they are a shape rather than a ledger, and the shape is the entire point of drawing them.
- **The chart plots the headline quantity** because a chart sitting under a number and ending somewhere else would spend every reading being explained.
- **The accounts table totals to the headline** only because nothing is left out of it.
