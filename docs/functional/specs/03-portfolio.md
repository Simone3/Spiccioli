## 3.1 Behaviour

- **Failing-check banner.** Shown whenever any check fails, above everything else, with the count of failing checks and linking to [§9](09-checks.md). Absent when all pass.
- **Net worth is the headline** and is what the portfolio is worth in hand ([§11.4](11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth)): ordinary cash at its balance, holdings at their latest known price *less* the hypothetical capital-gains tax and sell fee of [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation), and pension funds *less* the hypothetical exit tax of the same section.
- **Five figures, in this order, and the lower four add up to the headline.** *Net worth* is the headline. The four beneath it are the decomposition of [§11.4](11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth):
  - **Cash** — Σ balances of every cash account that is not a pension fund. Money that is already money, at its face balance: no haircut, no cost basis and no rate anywhere in it.
  - **Securities at cost** — Σ holdings at what was paid for them, purchase fees included ([§11.1](11-calculations.md#111-weighted-average-cost)). What the ledger recorded going in, which is not what the positions are worth now.
  - **Unrealised net investment gain** — what selling every holding today would add to the portfolio or take out of it, net of the tax and the fees of [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation).
  - **Pension funds, net** — Σ balances of the pension fund accounts, after the exit tax of [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation). Contributions and the fund's own revaluation together, in one figure.
- `cash + securities at cost + unrealised net investment gain + pension funds, net = net worth`, **by construction**: the four cover every account exactly once ([§11.4](11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth)). The middle two are one brokerage account seen twice over — its balance is the sum of them — which is why they sit adjacent.
- **That is the only decomposition offered**, and it splits the portfolio by **what kind of claim each figure is**: money held, money put in, what the securities have made on it, and what the pension would leave after its tax. **Read down, the estimation accumulates** — the first two lines are what the ledger recorded, the last two are the two places [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation) is applied. **Neither of the first two is a sum of transactions**, and neither may be labelled as one: cash includes opening balances, which are not transactions, and securities at cost values holdings at their cost basis, which is not a transaction either.
- **A pension fund is one line rather than two**, though its contributions and its revaluation are both derived ([§2](02-domain-model.md)): the exit tax falls on the contributions and not on the revaluation, so there is no honest way to put part of it on a line called *gain* ([§11.4](11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth)). **It is the one asset that is not split into what went in and what it made**, and the reason is the tax rather than the data.
- **The headline figure is an estimate**, and **the note that says so is on the four lines beneath it rather than on the figure itself**. Each of the four carries its own note on hover, and the four together are the [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation) caveat split along the lines it actually applies to. **Two of the four say that nothing has been applied**, which is what makes *read down and the estimation accumulates* something the screen shows rather than something this document asserts:
  - **Cash** — nothing is estimated here. Every cash account that is not a pension fund, at its opening balance plus every transaction on it: no haircut, no cost basis and no rate. **Opening balances are in it, and an opening balance is not a transaction.**
  - **Securities at cost** — what the ledger recorded going in, purchase fees included ([§11.1](11-calculations.md#111-weighted-average-cost)). Not what the positions are worth now, and **not a sum of transactions**: a holding is carried at its cost basis, which no single row holds.
  - **Unrealised net investment gain** — an estimate. What selling every holding today would add to the portfolio or take out of it, after the capital-gains tax and the sell fee. **The tax is worked out one holding at a time, so a loss on one position does not reduce the tax on another, and the sell fee is charged once per holding — both of which push the figure down**, so the estimate is a cautious one rather than an optimistic one. The gross, untaxed picture is one screen away, on Investments ([§7.1](07-investments.md#71-holdings)), where the per-holding breakdown lives.
  - **Pension funds, net** — an estimate. The balance less the exit tax, which falls on what was paid in rather than on what the fund earned, at a rate typed by hand. **And it says what the haircut does not do**: a pension fund netted for tax is still money that cannot be had before retirement.
- **Every euro of estimation in the headline is on the last two lines**, and both move if a security's `taxRate`, a fund's `exitTaxRate` or an institution's sell fee changes ([§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)). The headline is their sum and has nothing to say that they do not.
- **Gains and costs, all time.** What the portfolio has gained and lost by being kept in banks and funds instead of as cash: **five lifetime figures and their total**. Four are the lifetime sums of the categories carrying the roles `bank fees`, `wealth tax`, `interest and dividends` and `value adjustment` ([§6.3](06-categories.md#63-category-list)); the fifth is realised gain from sales ([§11.2](11-calculations.md#112-realised-gain-on-a-sale)). **Nothing on this card is an estimate** — every line is a recorded fact, which is the whole of what it has and the card above it has not.
- **It reads in one order: what was taken, then what was added, then the total** — bank fees, wealth tax, interest and dividends, value adjustments, realised gain on sales, total. **The two negative lines lead** because the costs of being banked are the part nobody keeps a running figure for.
- **Value adjustments are mostly the pension fund's own revaluation**, and in a file with a decade of history they are the largest line on the card ([§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)). The role is legal on any cash account ([§6.3](06-categories.md#63-category-list)), so a term deposit's accrued interest lands here as well; **the line sums whatever carries the role and does not ask which account it was on.**
- **The total is the sum of the five and is the figure the card exists to produce.** It is the net effect of a decade of being banked and invested, against the alternative of having held the money as cash. **It is an amount and not a rate**, it is not net worth and it does not reconcile to anything else on the screen — the money it is measured against was never held ([§11.8](11-calculations.md#118-annualised-return) is where *how well* is answered, and it covers the investments only).
- **The exit tax and the unrealised gain are not on this card**, though both are money the institutions have made or will take. Both are [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation) estimates and both are on the card above; either one here would make the total half record and half projection.
- **Realised gain covers the sales that have one.** A sale in a (security, account) whose running quantity ever went below zero has no average cost to be measured against and therefore no realised gain at all ([§11.2](11-calculations.md#112-realised-gain-on-a-sale)); those sales are left out of the sum, and the card says how many it left out and links to checks 8 and 9, which name the trades ([§9](09-checks.md)). **The total carries the same omission and the same count**, being made of a line that omits them ([§11](11-calculations.md#11--calculations)).
- **Every figure on this screen counts closed accounts**, the lifetime figures and net worth alike ([§11.4](11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth), [§6.1](06-categories.md#61-report)) — a closing date marks an account and sorts it last, it does not remove it from the arithmetic.
- **Breakdown by type.** Cash accounts contribute under the account's type, **a pension fund at its net balance like everywhere else** ([§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)); holdings contribute under their *security's* type, at the same net value used for net worth. A brokerage account never appears as a slice of its own — it is entirely its holdings — so the types available are the five cash types plus the four security types ([§2](02-domain-model.md)). Closed accounts and their holdings are in it, like everywhere else, which is what keeps the slices adding to net worth. **Only types actually present get a slice**: nine is the ceiling, and the portfolio in the mockup happens to hold all nine. A file with no meal vouchers and no gold would draw seven.
- **The pie** carries one slice per type present, sized by its share, with the slice count at its centre and the same colour keying the list beside it. The list beside it carries the amounts and percentages.
- **A type can total less than nothing, and then the amount is shown and the share is not.** An overdrawn current account does it, and so does a holding worth less than the fee it would cost to sell ([§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)). The row keeps its place in the list and shows the negative amount, but reads **0,0%** and is **given no slice**.
- **A type totalling exactly nothing is the same case**: it shows `€ 0,00` and **0,0%** and is given no slice. It is still a row — an emptied voucher balance or a term deposit that has just been paid out is a type the portfolio has and a shape it no longer contributes to.
- **Shares are computed against the total of the positive types**, not against net worth. The two figures differ by exactly the negative amounts, which are on screen a line away. **If no type is positive at all** the pie is replaced by a line saying the portfolio has no positive value to divide up, and the list still shows every amount.
- **Both are ordered by share, largest first**, and in the same order — the list reads down in the order the slices are drawn clockwise. Types with no share sort last, **by amount descending** — zero first, then the negatives from least to most. This is the one table in the application ordered by its own amounts (compare [§6.1](06-categories.md#61-report)).
- **Net worth over time**, monthly points from the start of the file to today, plus one at today ([§11.5](11-calculations.md#115-net-worth-over-time)). **It plots the same figure as the headline** — net of the hypothetical tax and sell fee, at each date rather than only at today's — so its last point is the headline, to the cent.
- Any month in which at least one holding had to be valued at cost is drawn dashed and labelled as cost-based; the line goes solid at the first month in which every holding had a price. **Only a month earlier than a security's first recorded price can be dashed** — a security with no price at all is worth zero on this line exactly as it is on the card above, which is what leaves the final point equal to the headline to the cent and means today's point is never dashed ([§11.5](11-calculations.md#115-net-worth-over-time)). The dashed stretch is the only part that is not net worth as computed everywhere else, and the legend also says that the rate and the fee applied across the whole line are today's, those being the only ones the file records ([§11.5](11-calculations.md#115-net-worth-over-time)).
- **Breakdown by account** lists every account with its computed balance — **brokerage and pension fund accounts at the net figures of [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)**, like everywhere else — **in the order of [§4.1](04-accounts.md#41-accounts)**: institution name, then opening date, then account name, with accounts belonging to no institution first and closed ones dimmed and last. Its total is the headline net worth. This and the Accounts screen are the two places every account is shown together, and neither filters ([§2](02-domain-model.md)). Read-only — accounts are created, edited and closed on the Accounts screen ([§4.1](04-accounts.md#41-accounts)).
- **Four columns and no more: institution, account, type and balance.** The account's notes are on the Accounts screen and nowhere else, which is the exact counterpart of that screen carrying **no balance column** ([§4.1](04-accounts.md#41-accounts)): each of the two shows the stored record or the computed figure, and neither shows both.
- **The Balance column carries a note on hover saying how the figure is arrived at** ([§11.4](11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth)): the opening balance plus every transaction on the account; a pension fund after its exit tax; a brokerage account, which holds no money of its own, at the net value of its holdings after the tax and the sell fee of [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation); and closed accounts computed like any other, **which is the reason the column totals to the headline**. It is the one column on the screen whose figure means three different things depending on the row it is in.

---

## Why it is this way

- **The decomposition separates money held from money put in, and both from the estimates laid on top of them.** An earlier version ran the first two together on one line called *cash and securities at cost*, and that was a compound name for a compound figure: a bank balance added to a cost basis, answering two questions with one number and admitting as much in its own title. Splitting them costs a line and no arithmetic — both halves were already inside it, and both are already summed for [§11.4](11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth) — and it leaves every line on the card a single kind of claim, which is what makes *read down and the estimation accumulates* a true description rather than an approximate one.
- **What is still not offered is a split by kind of asset.** That is the breakdown further down the same screen, and it is nine rows by type rather than four by nature; the two answer different questions and neither is the other at lower resolution. The one figure that does appear in both is *pension funds, net*, which is a line here and a slice there because a pension fund is the only account that is a whole asset class on its own — and it is the same number in both places, computed once.
- **The pension fund is a line of its own because folding it in was telling a lie about the rest.** With pension funds on the cash side, a decade of fund revaluations — recorded, real, and on the largest single position in most files — sat inside a figure labelled *at cost*, while the line labelled *unrealised gain* covered only the brokerage and quietly claimed to cover everything. A line of its own puts every euro of estimation where it actually is: the first two lines are what the ledger recorded, the third is what the securities estimate, the fourth is what the pension estimates. It is also the honest place to put an asset that is illiquid in a way none of the others is.
- **The headline is a liquidation figure and now says so consistently.** Netting the securities and not the pension made it neither market value nor liquidation value — an asset was being counted at face precisely because it was the one that could not be sold. It is still an approximation over sources that behave very differently, and the four notes below it are what carry that; what it is not any more is an approximation with one arbitrary exemption in it.
- **One note became four because the one was describing two different things at once.** A caveat on the headline had to cover the securities and the pension in a single paragraph, and every sentence in it was true of one of the two and not of the other — a reader wanting to know what had been done to *their pension figure* had to sort a mixed list. Four notes, each on the line it is about, say the same things in the place each is true. The gain is not brevity, which is unchanged; it is that **two of the four now say that nothing has been done at all**, and a note on *Cash* stating that there is no rate and no haircut anywhere in it is worth more than its absence, which reads identically to an oversight. It also puts *read down and the estimation accumulates* on the screen: hover the four in order and the notes go from *nothing is estimated here* to two estimates in a row.
- **The headline is left with no note of its own** because it would have had to be either a fifth thing to read or a pointer to the four below it, and a caveat that says *see below* is a caveat nobody reads twice. The figure is the sum of four lines; it holds no estimation that is not on two of them.
- **The all-time card was renamed and totalled because it was a list of four figures with no stated relationship.** *All time* said when they were measured and nothing about what they had in common, so the card read as a miscellany — three category sums and a derived one — and the reader was left to notice that all four are money the banks, the funds and the market added or took while the portfolio sat with them. Naming it *gains and costs* says what the four are, and summing them answers the question the four provoke and never used to answer: **how much better off is this than a decade of cash under a mattress?** The total is the card; the lines are its working.
- **Value adjustments were the missing line, and their absence was the largest omission on the screen.** A pension fund's decade of growth is recorded, real, and in most files bigger than the fees, the taxes, the dividends and the realised gains put together — and it appeared nowhere as a lifetime figure, being folded inside *pension funds, net* on the card above and inside the Investments group on the report ([§6.1](06-categories.md#61-report)). A card about what keeping money somewhere earned that omitted the largest thing it earned was answering its own question wrongly.
- **The total is a sum of recorded facts and would stop being one if either estimate joined it.** The unrealised gain and the pension exit tax are the two obvious candidates and both were declined: the first is what the securities *would* make, the second is what the fund *would* pay, and a single figure mixing what happened with what would happen has no reading — it is neither a record nor a forecast, and it would move every time a price was entered. The estimates already have a card, one card up, and the split between the two is now the difference between them.
- **The realised-gain total states its omissions** because a total that silently omitted the sales with no cost basis would be the one figure on that card that is not what it says it is. **The card total repeats the count rather than inheriting it quietly**, since a figure built on an incomplete line is incomplete by exactly as much.
- **Closed accounts stay in every figure** because a closed account should be an empty one — check 11 fails by name while it is not ([§9](09-checks.md)) — so in a file whose checks pass it contributes nothing to net worth anyway, and in a file whose checks do not it contributes the balance that is really still there. The bank charged those fees, that interest was received, that sale really was made, and money left in a shut account is money the file still says you have.
- **Three of the card's four roles are read by this card and by nothing else** — `bank fees`, `wealth tax` and `interest and dividends`, and no check consults any of them ([§9](09-checks.md)). One of the three is *named* once more: [§11.6](11-calculations.md#116-derived-matching) says a transfer fee belongs in a category carrying `bank fees`, which is a rule about how to record rather than something the application reads. The category list is fixed ([§6.3](06-categories.md#63-category-list)), so the card could have named its categories directly and been correct forever — but every other part of the application that cares about a particular kind of money keys off `role`, and one screen quietly matching on names would have been the exception that made the rule untrue. Roles cost nothing and keep the promise whole.
- **The fourth, `value adjustment`, is the one the card shares**, and it is read three ways: check 10 prompts a revaluation on a pension fund that has not had one ([§9](09-checks.md)), [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation) splits a fund's balance on it to find the exit-tax base, and this card sums it over the life of the file. **The three readings agree because they are the same sum taken over different scopes**, which is what makes adding the line free: the figure was already being computed, per account, for the arithmetic one card up.
- **Nothing on the card is counted twice, and one recording habit could make that untrue.** The five lines are four disjoint roles and a figure derived from trades rather than transactions, so no euro reaches two of them — except where a term deposit's accrued interest is entered as a value adjustment and then, on payment, entered again as interest ([§6.3](06-categories.md#63-category-list)) without the adjustment being reversed. That is a mis-recording rather than a defect in the card, no check reports it, and it is named here because the card is the one screen on which it would show up as a figure that is too large.
- **A negative or zero type keeps its amount and loses its slice** because a negative arc cannot be drawn, and pretending otherwise would leave a circle whose parts sum to more than the circle; a slice of no size is not a slice. The amount is the whole reason anyone would want the row, and a row saying so is worth more than a row that vanished.
- **Shares are computed against the positive types** so the column keeps adding to 100% while a negative type sits in it.
- **The pie and the list share one order** so that finding a slice in the legend is following a line rather than searching, and the whole column reads downwards from the largest contribution to the deepest hole. The pie exists to make the shape of the portfolio readable at a glance. This is the one table sorted by its own amounts because there are nine rows, they are a shape rather than a ledger, and the shape is the entire point of drawing them.
- **The chart plots the headline quantity** because a chart sitting under a number and ending somewhere else would spend every reading being explained.
- **The breakdown by account totals to the headline** only because nothing is left out of it. **It borrows [§4.1](04-accounts.md#41-accounts)'s order rather than inventing one**, since it is the same twelve rows the Accounts screen shows and a reader who has learnt where a bank sits on one screen should not have to learn it again on the other. There is no third order in the application for the same list.
- **It is called a breakdown because that is what it is**, and calling it *Accounts* made it look like the Accounts screen rendered small. The screen above it splits net worth four ways by nature and further up by type; this splits the same figure by the account the money is in, and the three headings now read as three cuts of one number rather than as two breakdowns and a list.
- **The notes column went because a note is a property of the account and not of the balance.** It was the one column on the screen that was neither a figure nor the label of one — *matures 03/2027*, *household bills*, *moved everything to Fineco* — and it belongs where the account is edited ([§4.1](04-accounts.md#41-accounts)), which is one click away and is the only place it can be changed. Dropping it leaves four columns that are all about the same arithmetic, and it takes the widest and most variable column out of a table that has to fit beside a nine-row pie. **It also makes the pair of screens symmetrical**, which they nearly were already: Accounts holds every stored field and refuses to compute a balance, and this table computes the balance and holds no stored field beyond the three that name the row.
- **The Balance column is the one that needed explaining, and it now does it in place.** It is the only figure in the application that is computed three different ways depending on the row — a sum of transactions, a sum less a tax, a sum of holdings' net proceeds — and until now a reader who wondered why a brokerage account showed a figure no statement of theirs carried had to find [§11.3](11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation) to learn why. The note is on the header rather than on each row because it is one rule with three branches, not twelve facts, and **it is the same move as the four notes on the net worth card**: the explanation sits on the thing it explains.
