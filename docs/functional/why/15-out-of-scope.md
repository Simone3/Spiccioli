# §15 — Out of scope for v1 — why it is this way

*[Index](../README.md) · [the specification](../specs/15-out-of-scope.md)*

---

- **Currency** is a version of its own: a second one needs a rate table and a decision at every total about what is being added to what — and a one-valued field stored in advance would not have bought that version anything. **Refusing a non-EUR quote is not a step towards it**: it is the opposite, a door closed so that the premise stays true. Everywhere else the premise holds because nothing could carry another currency; a fetch is the one path by which a number the user did not type reaches an amount, so it is the one place the premise has to be defended rather than assumed ([§7.6](../specs/07-investments.md#76-prices)).
- **Debts are absent from the premise rather than from the model**, which is the distinction worth keeping. Nothing in [§11.4](../specs/11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth) assumes a balance is positive — it sums signed figures over every account, and an overdrawn one already contributes a negative. What is missing is a type that means *this is owed*, and the reason not to add one now is that a debt has a schedule, an interest rate and a remaining term, none of which an account holds and all of which someone would immediately want.
- **Inflation is declined because a real-terms figure needs a source of truth the file has no way to hold.** It would mean an index, kept up to date from somewhere, and a decision at every figure about which year's euros it is quoted in — including on the net worth chart, where the honest version restates every point and the resulting line is a different shape from the one above it. For a ledger whose job is to say what is there, nominal is the right unit, and the arithmetic of comparing 2016 with 2026 is one the reader can do knowing what the figures are.
- **A time-weighted return would answer a question this file is not for.** It measures how the investments performed independently of when money was put in, which is the right way to judge a fund and the wrong way to judge what happened to a person's savings — and it is the one of the two that the data cannot support, since it needs the whole portfolio valued on every purchase date and the price history is a decade of weekly-ish records with gaps. The money-weighted figure is both the more useful one here and the one that is free ([§11.8](../specs/11-calculations.md#118-annualised-return)).
- **TFR with the employer is the largest thing this file can be silently missing**, and it is worth naming for that reason alone. Every other omission here is a refinement of a figure that exists; this one is an asset that does not appear at all, on a screen whose headline claims to be net worth. It is out of scope because recording it properly means a monthly accrual and a statutory revaluation — an account whose balance the application would have to *compute* rather than record, which nothing else here does ([§1](../specs/01-premise-and-constraints.md)).
- **The net-to-gross ratio was removed, and the whole difficulty around the gross line went with it.** The figure was a percentage nobody acted on: the wedge between gross and net is set by tax law, it barely moves from month to month, and no decision on any screen turned on it. It was also **the only figure in the application that divided an exactly-defined number by one copied off a document** — `netSalary`, defined to the euro by [§11.7](../specs/11-calculations.md#117-salary-figures), over whatever an employer's payroll prints as *totale lordo*. That mismatch was the entire reason the composition of that line mattered, and it mattered nastily: reimbursements inside the line push the ratio down, a car deduction reducing the line pushes it up, so a payslip carrying both yields a perfectly ordinary-looking number that is wrong at both ends, with no check able to see it.
- **What is left is a much smaller thing, and it needs no test and no correction.** `Σ gross`, `yearAvgGross` and `grossPerHour` are a sum, a mean and a rate over one field, so they are consistent with each other and comparable across years whatever the line contains. The residue is that they are not comparable with somebody else's idea of gross pay — a level effect, visible, and nothing like an error that cancels itself.
- **Deleting the figure was cheaper than defining it**, which is the general lesson worth keeping: the two-payslip test, the recorded alternative formula and the note about which convention an employer uses were all machinery in service of a number nobody was going to read. Detecting the convention automatically was never on the table anyway — the only evidence is `gross` against `contractGross` across months, and bonuses, overtime and part-months move that difference for reasons that have nothing to do with the question.
- **A fabricated opening trade reaches much further than it looks**, which is why it is recorded even though the file this was built for needs none. That single invented purchase sets `avgCost`, and through it the invested total, the gain, the hypothetical tax and **the realised gain on every later sale in that position** ([§11.1](../specs/11-calculations.md#111-weighted-average-cost), [§11.3](../specs/11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)); its date decides where the net worth chart begins, the line starting at the earliest of any opening date, transaction or trade ([§11.5](../specs/11-calculations.md#115-net-worth-over-time)); and it has no bank transaction behind it, so **check 6 reports it for as long as the file exists** ([§9](../specs/09-checks.md)). **Nothing marks it and no check can name it as invented** — it is a purchase like any other, which is exactly the problem. What would fix it is an opening position on a holding: a quantity, an average cost and a date, recorded as a statement about where the history starts rather than as a trade that happened, which keeps it out of check 6 and out of realised-gain arithmetic. It is the same shape `openingBalance` already has for a cash account, and it is a version's work.
- **Accrued interest is left out because the alternative is a figure that ages.** A `Value adjustment` on a term deposit is already legal and already counts ([§6.3](../specs/06-categories.md#63-category-list)); what is declined is anything that computes or prompts for one, since a prompt would need the rate, the compounding and the maturity date, and the last of those is deliberately not tracked either. Under-reading a term deposit until it pays is a known and bounded error in the safe direction.
- **Another tax system** is a change to [§11.3](../specs/11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation) alone, since the rate is already a parameter on the instrument; anything beyond that is future work.
- **Individual bonds: why the type is absent rather than merely unsupported, and what adding it would take.** A `Bond` type that existed and half worked would be worse than no type at all, because the file would hold a wrong cost basis and two checks would fail forever with nothing on screen saying why. There are three problems and the second has no workaround.
  - **A bond is quoted as a percentage of face value, not in euros per unit.** You hold *€ 10.000 nominal* of a BTP and the screen says `98,35`, meaning 98,35% of face — € 9.835. The trade record computes `quantity × unitPrice`, which gives € 983.500. Entering the price as `0,9835` gets the total right and leaves the price field holding a figure no statement or website prints, with only two decimals of the real quote left inside `decimal(4)`; entering the quantity as `10` lots of € 1.000 is wrong in the other direction. **Neither survives *Update prices***, which writes the provider's `98,35` straight into a Price record and therefore into net worth, out by a factor of a hundred, silently ([§7.6](../specs/07-investments.md#76-prices)).
  - **The cash that moves is not the price.** A bond pays its coupon once or twice a year, so a buyer part-way through a coupon period repays the seller the interest accrued so far — the buyer will collect the whole coupon later. Buying € 10.000 nominal at 98,35 six months into a 3% year costs **€ 9.835 of bond plus about € 150 of accrued interest**, and € 9.985 leaves the account. `total = qty × price + fees` cannot express that, so **check 6 fails on every bond purchase in the file, permanently**, and check 7 on every sale. There is no way to record around it: folding the accrued interest into the price corrupts the cost basis and the price history, and recording it as a separate transaction breaks the one-trade-one-transaction pairing that checks 6 and 7 are ([§11.6](../specs/11-calculations.md#116-derived-matching)).
  - **A bond ends.** At maturity the issuer repays face value and the position is gone. There is no redemption event; the nearest thing is a sale at exactly 100, which works but is prompted by nothing — a matured bond sits in the portfolio until check 3 notices the price has gone stale.
  - **And not every bond is the same shape.** A BOT or another zero-coupon has no accrued interest at all, but its whole return is taxed as *interest* rather than as a capital gain, which is a different calculation again and not the one [§11.3](../specs/11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation) performs.
  - **What it would take**, for whoever picks this up: a per-100 rule on the trade total, the cost basis, the liquidation estimate and the chart, driven off the type; a price column and price editor that mean *per 100 nominal* on some rows and *per unit* on others; and an `accruedInterest` field on the trade that goes **into the cash total and stays out of the cost basis**, because it is a prepaid coupon and not part of what the bond cost. That last rule is the delicate one — it is wrong quietly rather than loudly — and it is the reason this is a version's work rather than an afternoon's.
  - **Dropping the type costs nothing today** and is reversible: no file holds a `Bond`, and adding the type back is additive rather than a migration.
- **The four tax simplifications are declined together, and for one reason.** Loss offsetting, carried-forward losses, the gross-of-expenses rule and the flat sell fee are all refinements to a figure that is **already labelled an estimate on every screen it appears on** ([§11.3](../specs/11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)). Two of them err towards understating net worth, one is worth cents, and one is already right for the instruments most of the money is in. What they have in common is that the cheap-looking ones are not cheap: offsetting and a per-order fee both turn the tax from something a holding has into something the portfolio has, which then has to be handed back to individual holdings so that account balances and the breakdown by type still add up ([§11.4](../specs/11-calculations.md#114-balances-and-net-worth), [§3.1](../specs/03-portfolio.md#31-behaviour)). **The estimate is allowed to be an estimate**; what is not allowed is for it to be wrong in a way nothing on the screen admits, which is why each of them is written down with the direction it errs in.
- **The pension fund's opening balance sits outside that group**, and is the counter-example that makes the point. It is not a refinement of a tax rule but a missing input — one number off a statement, feeding two halves the model already derives ([§11.3](../specs/11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)) — so it is the only known simplification here that does not turn a property of one holding into a property of the whole file, and the only one that costs a field rather than a shape. It is also the only one whose error is **zero on the file this was written for** and unbounded on somebody else's, which is exactly why it is written down instead of assumed away.
- **Carried-forward losses are the one of the four worth building first**, if any of them ever is, because it is the only one that is not a refinement of an existing figure: it is an asset the file does not know about, with an expiry date the file cannot warn about, and a value the user could act on. **Cheapest first is a different order** and puts the pension field ahead of all of them.
- **A same-day round trip is not marked** because the marker would appear on a correct figure far more often than on a distorted one — the ordering rule exists precisely so that the ordinary same-day buy-and-sell is an ordinary round trip — and a caveat that mostly fires on nothing teaches people to ignore it.
- **The pension fund assumption is recorded rather than parameterised** because a setting saying which way a fund credits would be a control the user has to get right to make a check work, on a question they can answer only by watching the check fail. The recorded sketch degrades to the present behaviour on its own, which is the property that makes it worth writing down and not worth building yet.
- **Bulk import of trades** is declined because pasting is for bank exports, which arrive by the hundred every month ([§5.7](../specs/05-transactions.md#57-bulk-import)). Trades arrive a few dozen a year, one confirmation at a time, and each one carries a security that may not exist yet — the paste would need a column for that too, and a way to say what to do when it does not match.
- **Bulk import of payslips** is the same reasoning, more so: a dozen a year, ten fields each, and no export to paste from — a payslip is read off a PDF by eye whatever the application offers.
- **Category groups** would add a concept for no gain.
- **Automatic generation between trades and transactions** is refused because the independence of the two sources is what makes the checks meaningful.
- **A warning tier** would become a place for things to sit unfixed.
- **Bulk edit** is refused because rules do the same job repeatably and leave a reason behind.
- **Transferring a position between brokers** is not modelled, and the two failing checks are the price of not building it.
- **Corporate actions: what was tried, what each attempt breaks, and why deleting the position is the answer.** This is worth recording in full, because “enter it as an adjusting trade” is the obvious idea, it was the earlier answer here, and **it does not work**.
  - **A forward split cannot be entered at all.** A 2-for-1 split wants a purchase of the extra shares at a price of nothing: quantity doubles, `costBasis` is untouched, `avgCost` halves, which is exactly right ([§11.1](../specs/11-calculations.md#111-weighted-average-cost)). But `unitPrice` must be **greater than zero** ([§13](../specs/13-validation.md)), so the trade the arithmetic wants is the one trade the form refuses. Entering `0,0001` instead is not a workaround: it puts a small error into the cost basis of a position that is then wrong for as long as it is held, and silently.
  - **A reverse split is worse, and has no workaround.** It wants shares taken *away*, and the only record that removes shares is a sale — which does `costBasis −= q × avgCost` ([§11.1](../specs/11-calculations.md#111-weighted-average-cost)). A 1-for-10 reverse split entered that way destroys nine tenths of the cost basis, so the position looks as though it cost a tenth of what it did, and every figure derived from it is wrong in the flattering direction. It also books a **realised loss of nearly the whole position** ([§11.2](../specs/11-calculations.md#112-realised-gain-on-a-sale)), which is a fact about nothing that happened and lands in the *gains and costs* card on Portfolio ([§3.1](../specs/03-portfolio.md#31-behaviour)), the one card on that screen that is supposed to be a record rather than an estimate.
  - **Selling everything and buying it back has the same defect in a more expensive form**: it invents a realised gain for the whole position, and it leaves checks 6 and 7 with two trades and no bank transactions to pair them against ([§9](../specs/09-checks.md)).
  - **So the position is deleted and re-entered.** The realised gain on the sales that are deleted goes with them, which is the cost and is stated here rather than discovered later: a position that had sales before the event loses their contribution to the lifetime figure and to the card total it feeds ([§3.1](../specs/03-portfolio.md#31-behaviour)). Everything after the event is then ordinary. It is a handful of edits, for something that happens to one holding every few years, and it is the only route that leaves no wrong number behind.
  - **Relaxing `unitPrice > 0` was considered and declined.** It would make a forward split enterable and would still leave a reverse split with no representation, so it buys half a case at the cost of a validation rule that currently means something simple and true — a trade happened at a price. The whole feature is one version's work when it is wanted: an adjustment record that scales quantity and leaves the basis alone is what a split actually is, and it is not a trade.
- **Merging two securities** is a handful of edits for something that happens once.
- **An oversold position is not shown** because there is no reading of its figures that would be true, and checks 8 and 9 are the report the situation actually calls for.
- **A file is not re-validated on open** because the migration script is written once, for one file, by the one person who will run it.
- **The import stays strict about shape, and infers nothing at all.** What a row's characters mean is said by three controls the user can see and move ([§5.7](../specs/05-transactions.md#57-bulk-import)), and anything that does not fit what they say is reported as a row that cannot be read rather than guessed at. The two decorations that *are* read — a leading `+` and a `€`/`EUR` marker — are the two that cannot be mistaken for anything else, and a fourth column is tolerated because ignoring it cannot misread anything.
