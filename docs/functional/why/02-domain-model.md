# §2 — Domain model — why it is this way

*[Index](../README.md) · [the specification](../specs/02-domain-model.md)*

---

- **The cash/brokerage split is enforced out to every picker and filter** because offering the wrong kind of account could only produce an empty result or a record that cannot exist. Closed accounts are the exception both lists keep, since their rows stay editable ([§4.3](../specs/04-accounts.md#43-creating-and-editing)).
- **`Liquidity` became `Current account`, `Cash` and `Brokerage cash` because one name was covering three different places money sits**, and the name was its own giveaway: *liquidity* is a property of a balance rather than a kind of account, so the type described what the money was like instead of where it was. The three behave identically in every calculation — which is why they were ever one type — but they are not the same thing to read: the breakdown of [§3.1](../specs/03-portfolio.md#31-behaviour) was drawing a single slice of nearly a fifth of the portfolio out of a salary account, a wire sitting uninvested at a broker and some notes in a drawer, and that slice answered no question anybody has.
- **The split is worth three types rather than a note in the account's `notes` field** for three concrete returns. It gives the breakdown a slice per kind of place. It gives [§11.6](../specs/11-calculations.md#116-derived-matching) a name for the account a purchase is funded out of, which had to be described in a sentence before. And it makes **institution required on every type but one** — under the old enum the field had to be optional across the whole of `Liquidity` so that physical cash could exist, which left the one account in the file that genuinely has no bank indistinguishable, on the form, from a current account whose bank nobody had filled in yet ([§13](../specs/13-validation.md)).
- **Nothing keys off which of the three an account is, and that was a decision rather than an omission.** The obvious use for `Brokerage cash` would be to make check 6 pair a trade against its own broker's cash leg instead of against any account at the shared institution ([§11.6](../specs/11-calculations.md#116-derived-matching)) — and it would fail on every file where the current account and the trading account are one account, which at most Italian banks they are. The type stays a label, so recording one wrongly costs a slice in the wrong place and never a check.
- **A pension fund carries a rate of its own because it is taxed on a different thing.** A security's rate applies to a *gain*; a fund's applies to what was *paid in*, the returns having been taxed year by year inside the fund already ([§11.3](../specs/11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)). Splitting the account's transactions into contributions and revaluation is what makes that base derivable, and it costs nothing to derive: the `value adjustment` role already marks every row that is a revaluation ([§6.3](../specs/06-categories.md#63-category-list)), because check 10 needed to find them anyway. **Without the split the only available base would have been the whole balance**, which taxes the fund's returns a second time and understates the account by the rate times its lifetime gain — on the one account where that gain has had a decade to accumulate.
- **The tax rate sits on the security rather than on its type** because `Bond ETF` settles nothing: two of them side by side carry two different rates, since the reduced government rate applies to whatever share of the fund is government paper and the ordinary one to the rest — so the blend is a fact about that fund and not about the category it is in. Putting the rate on the instrument leaves `type` to do the one job it is good at, which is grouping the portfolio into slices a person recognises.
- **`Bond` is not one of the types, and that is a v1 decision rather than an oversight** ([§15](../specs/15-out-of-scope.md)). An individual bond does not fit the trade record at all: it is quoted as a percentage of face value rather than in euros per unit, and the cash that moves on a purchase includes the interest accrued since the last coupon, which is neither part of what the bond cost nor something `qty × price + fees` can express. Offering the type without the fields would produce a wrong cost basis and two checks failing forever on every bond in the file. A `Bond ETF` has neither problem — it is a fund, quoted per unit in euros, with no accrued interest and no maturity — so bond exposure is available and only the instrument is not.
- **A day's price is overwritten rather than versioned** so that the last word on a day wins and correcting a typo does not leave a second record to disambiguate. **`source` says who had that last word**, which is the only question the field is there to answer: a figure the user typed and a figure a provider returned are worth telling apart when reading a decade of history, and a fetched value that has since been corrected by hand is the user's figure now, not the provider's. It changes nothing — no check, no total and no fetch consults it ([§7.6](../specs/07-investments.md#76-prices)) — so it can be a label without becoming a rule.
- **The category invariant is what lets every total read the stored value** instead of re-deriving it behind each figure ([§6.1](../specs/06-categories.md#61-report), [§9](../specs/09-checks.md)). An upgrade is included in the list of moments that restore it because a new version may seed a category the old one did not have or change what an existing one means, and the invariant would otherwise hold for every file except the ones just upgraded ([§12](../specs/12-storage.md)).
- **Recording the tax withheld on a sale** is what lets the sale's `total` equal the bank transaction it is compared against in check 7, and what makes realised gain a real figure rather than a pre-tax one. The hypothetical rate of [§11.3](../specs/11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation) is an estimate and has no bearing on a sale that actually happened.
- **A payslip carries no payment date** because the bank transaction already carries the real date, and duplicating it here would create a second figure to keep in step. **The three parts of a pension contribution are three fields rather than one** because that is how the money moves: the fund credits them separately, so recording them separately lets check 5 pair each figure with the credit that carries it, on exactly the shape check 4 already uses. One combined figure would have forced a comparison of monthly totals instead, in which two adjacent months' windows overlap and compete for the same credit.
- **Categories are records rather than constants** for two reasons. Transactions reference a stable `id`, so the migration script and the file format have something durable to point at; and every check that concerns a particular kind of money keys off `role` rather than off a name, so nothing breaks when a label is reworded and a future version can add, say, a second salary-like category without touching [§9](../specs/09-checks.md).
- **An oversold position is not valued at all** because more sold than was ever bought is not a position that can be valued — there is no meaningful average cost, no invested total and no gain to derive from it. It is an anomaly and not a stage of ordinary work: the usual causes are a purchase that was never entered and a date that was mistyped.
- **A dip disqualifies the position even after a recovery** because the sale drew the cost basis down by more than was in it, and no later purchase puts that back ([§11.1](../specs/11-calculations.md#111-weighted-average-cost)). 50 bought, 100 sold and 200 bought later ends at a quantity of 150, which is positive and still wrong by an amount nothing on the screen would reveal. Presenting an impossible position in the tables would mean inventing a reading for every figure on the screen, in the service of a state whose only correct next step is to fix it ([§15](../specs/15-out-of-scope.md)).
- **All three quantities are shown** because `quantity` is a difference and a difference forgets: 338 bought and none sold and 900 bought and 562 sold are the same position and not the same history, and the second is the one where the weighted average is worth reading twice.
