# §13 — Validation — why it is this way

*[Index](../README.md) · [the specification](../specs/13-validation.md)*

---

- **Why the four exceptions are prevented rather than reported.**
  - **Uniqueness:** the damage is to the pickers, and it is immediate — two identical entries in a list the user is about to choose from cannot be told apart, so the next record is misfiled by a form that offered no way to get it right. A check would report the collision after everything chosen in between had already gone to the wrong one of the two.
  - **Deletion with dependents:** the alternative is not a reported inconsistency but a dangling reference — a transaction on an account that no longer exists. The file has no state for that and no screen could render it.
  - **The two locks:** both would silently restate records already written rather than correct the one in front of the user — a cash account turned brokerage orphans every row on it, a purchase turned sale reverses a position. Deleting and re-entering is the honest way to do either, and it is available. **The account lock has no precondition, deliberately.** It would have been narrower to let it bite only once the account holds a transaction or a trade, and the extra reach costs nothing: an account created as the wrong kind and noticed straight away is deleted and re-entered in the same breath, there being nothing in it to lose. What the precondition cost was worse — `openingBalance` is forced to 0 on a `Brokerage` account ([§2](../specs/02-domain-model.md)), so an empty `Current account` carrying an opening balance could be switched across and lose that figure with no record deleted and nothing on screen having refused. A lock that holds from the first second is also one sentence rather than two, and one the user can rely on without first working out what the account currently contains. Note how narrow it still is: the seven cash types stay interchangeable forever, `Brokerage cash` among them — it sits on the cash side of the boundary whatever its name suggests ([§2](../specs/02-domain-model.md)). For five of them nothing at all is derived from which one an account is — a savings account recorded as `Current account` that should have been a `Deposit account` is a slice in the wrong place on one screen ([§3.1](../specs/03-portfolio.md#31-behaviour)) and nothing else. **Two of the seven carry a consequence, and in both cases the consequence is a single field on the form in front of the user.** `Pension fund` is the type that takes an exit tax off the balance ([§11.3](../specs/11-calculations.md#113-hypothetical-liquidation)), so moving an account into it asks for a rate and moving one out clears it; `Cash` is the type that has no institution ([§2](../specs/02-domain-model.md)), so moving an account into it clears that field and disables it, and moving one out re-enables it empty and asks for it. Both are visible as they happen and reversible by setting the type back. That is a correction the user can see, which is what separates it from the two locks above, where the record being restated is not the one in front of them.
  - **Contract dates against payslips:** both records are on screen as it is typed — the contract is the selector scoping the whole screen ([§8.1](../specs/08-salaries.md#81-payslips)) — so this is not a rule about a record the user has not reached yet. A payslip from before you were hired is always a typo, never a half-entered state on the way to something. Enforcing only the first direction would have left the second as the way around it: the same file state, reached by editing the contract instead of the payslip, and reached silently, since the per-year table is built from the contract's dates and would simply stop showing the years it had orphaned.
- **The thread through all four is that the counterpart record is already chosen, already on screen, and not going to arrive later.** Where that is not true — a trade whose funding transaction has not been entered yet, a transfer whose second leg comes with next month's export, a sale whose purchase is further down the pile of confirmations — the rule is a check, because a form that refuses those is a form blocked by a record the user is on their way to entering. That is also why an oversell is saved: refusing it would mean a form that can be blocked by a record the user has not reached yet, on the one screen where trades are entered in whatever order the confirmations came out of the envelope.
- **Every record is changed by a form, and no table cell is edited where it sits.** The earlier design edited five tables in place — transactions, both trade tabs, the price history and the per-year working days — and each one needed its own rule for a refusal that arrives after the value has been typed: the cell stays open, the previous value stands, the row is never half-changed. That is a second validation model running beside the forms', reached by a control that looks like text until it is clicked, and written out five times over. A form has one model: the fields are refused as they are typed, what cannot be judged until the whole record is known disables the save, and abandoning it changes nothing. Making that the only model deletes the second one outright, and it makes a record's fields visible together — the account, the date and the category of a transaction being things read against each other rather than one at a time through a column.
- **The cost is a click on the fast paths**, and it is the honest cost of the trade: correcting one category on one row after an import is now *Edit*, a picker and *Save* rather than two clicks in the cell. What makes that acceptable is that volume was never the cell's job — changing many categories at once is what rules are for ([§6.2](../specs/06-categories.md#62-rules)), and they leave a reason behind where a cell leaves nothing.
- **Offering only valid choices is the general shape of every rule here that could have been a refusal and is instead an absence.**
- **`notes` is stated once here instead of in nine tables**, being the one field with no rule of its own.
- **The paste and the form admit the same values because the paste is no longer guessing.** The earlier design required two decimals on every pasted amount, which was the price of inferring the separator from the digits; with the separators declared on the import screen there is nothing to infer, so the requirement bought nothing and cost a reshaped export on every import ([§5.7](../specs/05-transactions.md#57-bulk-import)). What guards the column now is the grouping rule: a thousands separator that does not fall in threes, or one present when the control says none, is a row that cannot be read rather than a figure out by a factor of a thousand.
- **There is no currency field** because a picker with one entry would have been a control that can only be confirmed, on two forms, read by nothing — and the version that adds a second currency has to revisit every total in [§11](../specs/11-calculations.md) regardless, which a field stored in advance does not help with ([§15](../specs/15-out-of-scope.md)).
- **The field rules are not re-run on open** because they exist to stop a person mistyping, not to defend against a malformed file: the ten years of history arrive through a one-off migration script written for this file alone ([§12](../specs/12-storage.md)), and a script that writes something the forms would have refused has produced data the application will show faithfully and the checks will complain about. That is the intended outcome.
- **An institution's sell fee has no empty state** so that a fee of nothing is a fee that was typed.
- **Two accounts at one bank may not share a name** because two identical rows in a picker are indistinguishable; two *Conto Corrente* at different banks are not.
- **An account requires an institution unless it is `Cash`** because a current account, a broker's cash leg, a deposit, a term deposit, a pension fund, a voucher balance and a securities dossier are all held *by* somebody, and a brokerage account with no institution could never pair a trade with the money that paid for it ([§11.6](../specs/11-calculations.md#116-derived-matching), checks 6 and 7). Physical cash is the one kind of money nobody holds for you, and it now has a type of its own that says so — which is what turns the rule from *usually required* into *required, with one type it cannot apply to* ([§4.3](../specs/04-accounts.md#43-creating-and-editing)).
- **Opening and closing dates are barred from the future** because an account is opened before it is recorded, not after, and an account closing next month is an account that is still open.
- **A transaction's date is barred from the future** because a ledger records what has happened, and because it is what lets the net worth chart end exactly on the headline figure ([§11.5](../specs/11-calculations.md#115-net-worth-over-time)). A trade's date is barred for the same two reasons — it is a confirmation of something that happened, and a position dated ahead would put the chart below the headline it ends at.
- **A zero transaction amount is legal** because a card verification, a reversed charge and a fee waived to nothing all post as `0,00`, and refusing them would send the user to invent a figure the bank did not use.
- **A security's type stays freely editable** because it groups the portfolio breakdown ([§3.1](../specs/03-portfolio.md#31-behaviour)) and nothing is derived from it, so correcting one restates a slice and no recorded figure.
- **A price collision is never queried** because one price per security per day is the model and the last word on a day wins. A prompt per collision would fire every week, most often where a fetch replaces a value it wrote itself an hour earlier. What replaces it in each case is *visibility* rather than a question: the editor shows the value it is about to overwrite while the new one is being typed, and a fetch shows every value it would overwrite, once, before writing any ([§7.6](../specs/07-investments.md#76-prices)).
- **Saving the working-days form empty is the one unconfirmed delete** because nothing is lost but the number in the field, it is in front of the user as they clear it, and entering it again is the whole of the undo. A confirmation on top of a form the user is already saving would be a second dialog guarding a single integer. Zero is refused because it is not a year, it is a division by zero spelled differently.
- **`netPayment` takes either sign because a payslip can genuinely be negative**, and a validation that refused one would be a form blocking a document the user is holding. The ordinary cause is the December *conguaglio*, where the year-end recalculation of income tax turns up a debt larger than the month's net — the payslip then shows a negative figure, which the employer recovers later or carries forward. A month of largely unpaid leave with the car still being withheld does it too. **It is the only figure on the payslip that changes sign**, which is why the rest stay magnitudes: `gross` and `contractGross` are still required to be positive, and `refunds`, `carPayment` and the three contributions are still amounts whose direction [§11.7](../specs/11-calculations.md#117-salary-figures) supplies.
- **Nothing downstream needed changing for it**, which is the reason it was safe to relax. Check 4 compares the transaction against `netPayment` as a signed equality and always did ([§11.6](../specs/11-calculations.md#116-derived-matching)); a negative figure pairs with a debit of the same amount where the employer really did take the money back, and where the debt is carried to the next month instead there is no transaction and the check reports the payslip — which is the true state of affairs.
- **A payslip's label is neither required nor unique** because the ordinary monthly payslip is the one that has none.
- **A rule's substring is at least two characters** because a one-character rule would match most of the file on its first commit, and re-categorisation is immediate ([§6.2](../specs/06-categories.md#62-rules)). **A duplicate substring is flagged rather than refused** because the later rule can never fire, and saying so is more useful than refusing it. *Automatic* is not offered as a rule's category since a rule that assigns nothing is not a rule.
